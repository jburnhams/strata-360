## Milestone H: speed ramps and hyperlapse (idea 19 Oct; H1 and H3 built)

The planner uses only a small share of a long clip: between two windows of one clip, and after a clip's last window, the running is simply skipped by a cut. Idea: **race through that running instead of cutting it out**. A window followed by a long, quiet stretch plays its own moment at real speed, speeds up into a fast, stabilised hyperlapse through the stretch and slows back to real speed, landing where the next window of the clip starts, so the cut after it joins one continuous run. The film's length and the cuts on the beat do not change; only the footage a window plays does. The gyro and accelerometer the pipeline already reads are what make it stable.

**H1: speed ramps in the plan and the render (built 19 Oct, `edit/hyperlapse.py`).**
- *The ramp.* A ramp window has a lead at real speed (35% of the window, at least 1 s), a half-cosine speed-up (0.6 s), a hold at the hyperlapse speed, a half-cosine slow-down and a 0.3 s tail at real speed. The speed is solved so the window lands on the next window's start (`profile`; 3x to 24x); when the stretch is longer than 24x can cover, the ramp covers what it can and the cut after it skips the rest (`lands: false`). `offsets` is the exact integral of the speed, so the clip seconds at any film second are known in closed form.
- *What may be raced through.* A stretch is quiet when nobody talks in it (the clip's speech stretches), you did not skip it (a banned window or stretch), no other window of the film shows it, and it holds no unusable footage longer than 2 s; it must be at least 20 s long. Only windows that follow your heading (`follow_runner`) ramp: a view aimed at a person or at you cannot keep them in frame at 10x. A window the optimiser gave another technique is switched to `follow_runner` when that is one of its options or its length suits it, unless you chose its technique or it is locked. Speech windows, the script's dialogue windows, generated clips and windows under 3 s never ramp.
- *Stabilisation.* Rotation is already removed (every frame is stabilised by the gyro, and the heading the camera follows is smoothed in film time, so at 10x it is still calm). What the hyperlapse adds is the choice of source frames (`select_frames`, after Joshi et al. 2015): near each frame the speed asks for, the renderer may take a neighbour (up to 8 frames away), and a dynamic programme picks the run that keeps the stride in step (the accelerometer's magnitude, band-passed to the cadence, gives the stride's phase; frames at the top of the stride are preferred), avoids the frames where the gyro turns fastest (motion blur), and keeps the step steady. On synthetic running at 3 steps a second the bounce falls to a quarter at 20x and to two thirds at 12x: below one stride per film frame (about 17x at 50 fps) there are fewer frames at that phase than film frames, so the bounce can only be narrowed, not removed.
- *Where it is used.* `project.propose` and `plan_from_script` run the pass after the cuts are on the beat (and after the glides are chosen, for the script plan); the plan carries `ramp` and `src_dur_s` on the window, `utc_end` at the ramp's end, and the report in `plan.hyperlapse`. The final render picks the frames once per window (`FinalSource.source_times`) and decodes every frame it passes; the preview from the proxy takes the ramp's own times without the picker; the overlay's race clock races with the picture; the window's own sound fades out over the speed-up and the music carries the hyperlapse (`audio/mix.py`); the time map gives each window's real source span and maps any frame through the ramp. A cut into the window a ramp lands on says so (`transitions.py`). The setting `hyperlapse` (on by default) turns it off; the Timeline has a *Speed ramps* checkbox, a ⏩ on each ramp window and its speed and landing in the window column.
- *Done when:* unit tests on synthetic plans and telemetry (`tests/unit/test_hyperlapse.py`: the profile and its integral, landing and the speed cap, each blocker, the technique switch and its guards, the picker keeping the stride in step and moving forwards, the final and proxy sources, the time map, the sound's fade, the planner end to end with the setting on and off) and a real render of a synthetic clip (`tests/integration/test_render.py`: the race clock runs ahead with the ramp and the time map agrees). **Not yet checked on real footage:** how the hyperlapse looks on a Legends clip (stride, heading, exposure) and what it costs to render (the renderer decodes every frame it passes, so a 24x stretch decodes 24 frames per film frame).

**H3: longer ramps (built 19 Oct).** A ramp only had the window's own length (2 to 8 s), so at 24x a 4 s window covered about 40 s of running, and most quiet stretches between windows are minutes. Now a ramp window that cannot land in its own length *takes beats from its neighbours* in the film that show the same clip: the cut after it moves later onto a beat of the music (the next window then starts that much later in its clip, as a cut moved by `beat_sync` does, so its end and every other frame keep their film time, and the stretch it gave up is raced through too), and if that is not enough, the cut before it moves earlier (the window before ends sooner).
- The fewest beats that let the ramp land are taken; each neighbour keeps at least its technique's shortest length (2 s at least), and a ramp window grows to 16 s at most (about 5 min of running at 24x). The lead keeps the length it had, so the window's own moment is unchanged and every beat taken goes to the hyperlapse. When no choice lands, nothing is taken (H1's partial ramp).
- A neighbour gives no beats when it is locked, holds speech or the script's dialogue, is a generated clip or is a ramp window itself; nor does the next window when someone talks in the stretch it would give up.
- The beats are the music's real beats when the cuts were moved onto them (`project.film_beats`: the track's beat grid shifted by the sync's start), else the plan's grid. The film's length and its number of beats do not change; the report gives `took_s` per ramp.
- *Done when:* unit tests in `tests/unit/test_hyperlapse.py` (taking beats from the next window, then from the one before, the neighbours' floors, nothing taken when no choice lands or a neighbour may not give, the planner's beat times; ramps planned up front, each rule that stops one, the beam search giving them `follow_runner`, `violations` accepting them and the post-pass landing them). **Not yet checked on real footage**, with H2.
- *Planned up front (beat planner).* `chrono.plan_ramps` runs after the windows are cut: a window followed, before the next window of its clip, by a quiet stretch its own length cannot race through gets the fewest beats that let the hyperlapse land (16 s at most), and keeps the lead of its own length (`ramp_lead_s` on the segment). The longest stretches are planned first, the ramp windows hold at most a quarter of the film, and `rebalance` takes the beats from the other windows (if it cannot, the plan is made without them and says so). A planned ramp window always follows the heading and may be longer than `follow_runner`'s own range; it is planned only where a ramp is allowed (the setting is on, following the heading is not banned, you did not choose another technique for it, it is not locked or dialogue). `hyperlapse.apply` then ramps it with that lead, so it lands.
- *Not done:* the script plan does not plan ramps up front (its windows come from the script's items; there only the borrowing above applies), windows of one clip that are not next to each other in the film do not give beats, and a stretch after a clip's last window (nothing to land on) is not lengthened.
