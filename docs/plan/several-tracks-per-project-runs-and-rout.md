## Several tracks per project: runs and routes

A project holds any number of FIT / GPX tracks (`gps/tracks.py`: `tracks.json` manifest, files in `tracks/`; the older single `track.fit|gpx` counts as the first run, id `main`). Each is marked a **run** (the recording, needs times) or a **route** (a planned or official course, may have no times). The pipeline still sees exactly one track through `config.track_path`: the single run's own file, or `track.merged.npz` when there are two or more runs (`merge`: runs in priority order, a lower run only fills the stretches the higher ones did not record without a >30 s break; distance recomputed along the merged line). Routes are informational: only the overview map reads them. Points of interest come from GPX waypoints / named route points and FIT course points (`pois`). GPX is now read with the standard library (`gps/track.py::gpx_parts`; gpxpy was never a listed dependency), including `<rte>` files and heart rate / cadence / temperature extensions.

Server: `GET/POST/DELETE /api/tracks`, `POST /api/tracks/kind`, `GET /api/tracks/line` (any track or `merged`); `/api/track*` is unchanged and serves the race track. Web: a Tracks list in the track panel (multi-file add, run/route select, remove: files are moved to `tracks/removed/`, never deleted) and the overview map draws routes dashed, each run plain when there are several, the merged race track on top, and a marker with a tooltip for each point of interest.

Divergences (`tracks.divergences`): the race track is sampled every 10 m and compared with every route (distance to the nearest route point, routes filled in to 10 m spacing; a KD-tree). Stretches more than 50 m off all routes, less than 150 m apart, are one divergence; the 10 with the largest peak distance are listed in `GET /api/tracks` (`divergences`) and shown on the overview map as a red stretch with an exclamation mark at the farthest point (tooltip: peak distance, length, km of the run).

Divergences are direction-aware: each off-route sample's heading (from the points 30 m either side) is compared with the heading of the route where it is nearest, as lines (the way along it does not matter). A stretch with less than 30% of it heading more than 35° away from the route is only an offset alongside it and is left out; the rest are ranked by peak distance times the share heading the wrong way (`wrong`, `score`).

Route order and checkpoints (`tracks.route_order`): the routes are put in race order from the race track. A route is run in one stretch of the race track about as long as itself, which starts near one of the places the race passes the route's first point (a loop or a start and finish together make several) and goes on or back from there; each start and direction is tried and the stretch lying nearest the route is taken, and the routes are ordered by their stretches, each turned the way the race ran it. The list shows each route's number and the km of the run it covers; a numbered checkpoint is put halfway between the end of each route and the start of the next (they need not meet exactly), none at the start of the first or the end of the last. Checkpoints are in the `pois` of `GET /api/tracks` (`sym: 'checkpoint'`, `n`) and drawn as numbered blue markers.

Highlighting tracks: pointing at a track's row in the Tracks list (or at its line on the map) draws it on the map in bold yellow with a black outline, on top of everything, and shades the row; clicking the name (or the line) keeps it highlighted, several at a time, and clicking again lets go. Lines of tracks not yet drawn are fetched when first highlighted (`GET /api/tracks/line`).

Time at a checkpoint (`tracks.checkpoint_stops`): ONE visit is counted: the pass of the race track through a 300 m zone round the checkpoint that is nearest to where the two routes join along the race (a return to the same place after more than 2 minutes outside the zone is another visit and is not counted). Inside it the speed is taken over 30 s round each sample (a gap while the watch was paused counts as standing still); `arrived` is the first sample slower than 0.7 m/s and `left` the last one of the visit, so time still moving on the way in or out is not counted; `stopped_s` is the time between them. It is in the `stop` of each checkpoint in `GET /api/tracks` and shown in the checkpoint's hover card on the map (clock times in the project timezone).

Overlay clock (`overlay/layout.py::Clock`): top left, the time since the start of the race track in large digits (hours keep counting past 24: 61:01:01), under it the day of the race, and the date and time of day small. Days are calendar days in the project timezone, day 1 being the day the race starts and a new day at each local midnight; a first day shorter than an hour (a start after 23:00) is not a day of its own and goes on into the next calendar day (`race_day`). The distance counter is on the top line to the right of the elapsed time; before the run it reads 0.0 and after it the whole distance.

Run time split (`tracks.timing`): the run, from its first sample to its last, is cut at each checkpoint's arrival and departure (a checkpoint the run never slowed at is a moment). Each route's time is from leaving the previous checkpoint (the run's start for the first route) to arriving at the next (the run's end for the section it ended in); the checkpoint times and the route times add up to the run time exactly, and the Tracks list shows each with the total. Routes beyond the last checkpoint reached have no time; `consistent` is false if the checkpoints came out of order along the run.

Each route in the Tracks list also shows the distance actually run in it, the average pace (the route's time, without the checkpoints', over that distance) and its climb and descent (altitude with a 4 m hysteresis, as for the whole track); the total line gives the whole run's climb and descent.

Map end markers (`tracks.end_markers`): a green start marker at the first point of the run, a chequered finish-line marker at the end of the last route in race order, and a red marker at the last point of the run (tooltip: km and time). Without routes there is no finish line.

Overlay stage text (`overlay/layout.py::Stage`, schedule from `tracks.stage_schedule`): under the date, Before Race (with the time to the start, -4h3m), At Start (until the run is 300 m from where it began), Stage N (km so far / km of the whole stage, time so far / time of the whole stage; a stage runs from leaving the previous checkpoint to arriving at the next), Checkpoint N (km from the start of the run, time there so far / time of the whole stop), At Finish (only if the run came within 300 m of the end of the last route) and After Race (with the time since the end of the run, +34h4m). Distances are from the overlay's own distance counter, so they agree with the big distance figure. Without routes the stage is On Course.

Overlay climb line (`Climb`, `series.running_climb`): a small line under altitude and slope with the total ascent and descent so far (4 m wiggle filter on the smoothed altitude; 0 before the run, the totals after it). It can differ a little from the Tracks list, which uses the raw altitude.

Overlay maps: the whole-route map draws the whole route thin in the medium red, with the part already run over it in a darker red (a patch redrawn when the marker moves on, with the map's see-through-ness); the close-up map draws the route ahead paler and the route behind in the strong colour.

Checkpoint rows in the Tracks list work like the track rows: pointing at one rings that checkpoint on the map in bold yellow, clicking it keeps it highlighted (click again to let go).

Whole-route map lines and marker: lines are drawn at fractions of a pixel (`draw.line_mask`: supersampled), the whole route 1.35 px and the part run 1.65 px (was 1 and 2). The position marker on this map is an arrow (`draw.arrow`) that points the way the route goes on from the marker, looked at 12 map px ahead (not the way the last seconds went), in 5 degree steps; the close-up map keeps its round marker.

Route lines are full opacity: they are layers of their own over the (60% see-through) map pictures, on both maps, with their own `line_opacity` setting in race.json `overlay` (default 1.0). The arrow and the map outline already were.

The close-up map now matches the whole-route map: the whole route in the medium red and the part already run in the darker red, full-opacity lines (it keeps its own thicknesses, 2 and 3 px at 1080p), and the same arrow for the position, pointing along the route a short way ahead (9 map px; `route_bearing`), so it follows bends in the local route rather than the general direction used on the whole-route map (12 map px of a map of the whole race).

Stage text and the route (`tracks.stage_progress`, overlay `Stage`): a stage shows the distance run in it over the length of its route, `X/L km`. Each moment also has the route's own progress: the place on the route nearest the run while the run is within 50 m of it, held at the last such place while it is off the route. When the two differ by more than 10% (and 0.5 km), the route's progress comes first as the normal figure (`P/L km`) and the distance actually run follows in red (`ran X km`), as the bad part. The length and total time of a stage the run did not finish (the last one, no At Finish) are red too, since the run never got to the end of either.

Route distance beside the big distance (`_Ctx.route_elapsed`): next to the small `km` under the big distance counter, `route P / T km`: P is the distance covered along the routes so far, summed over the stages (completed stages in full, plus the progress along the route of the stage in hand, held where the run left the route), and T the total length of all the routes (including stages the run never reached). A run that strayed or missed a loop is behind the distance counter.

With routes, `route P / T km` replaces the small `km` label under the big distance, starting under the start of the big figure (and carrying the unit). Without routes (no route GPX) the overlay is as it was before routes: the small `km` label, and a stage shows the distance run in it over the distance run in the whole stage, all white.

Cut-offs on the overlay (`tracks.overlay_cutoffs`, `_Ctx.cutoff_ahead`): the cut-off of the control the race is heading for is always shown in red as a total time since the start (`CUT-OFF 40:00:00`) beside the day, under the elapsed time: the end of the stage in hand (the first before the start), the checkpoint while at it, the finish at and after it. In the stage line the stage's total time (only known afterwards) is no longer shown: it is the time so far, then, in red, the stage's cut-off counted from leaving the previous checkpoint. Cut-offs are typed per checkpoint and finish (`POST /api/tracks/cutoff`; the input box in the Tracks list is still to be built).

Cut-off box (Tracks list): each checkpoint row and the finish row have a small input; type the cut-off in any common form ("27h", "5:30 from last", "Sat 14:00") and press Enter or leave the box. It shows how it was read (total time, since the previous checkpoint, or time of day), the time since the start it comes to, and the time to spare (green) or late (red) when the run got there; a text that is not understood says why. On the overlay the stage line reads `01:23:45/10:00:00` (time in the stage / its cut-off, in red), the cut-off ahead is on its own line `CUT-OFF 10:00:00` under the elapsed time.

Correction: the `CUT-OFF` line under the elapsed time is the cut-off of the whole race (the finish's), always, not the next control's. In the stage line, with no cut-off set for the stage, the stage's total time is shown (white, `00:14:43/09:22:48`); with one, its cut-off in red replaces it.

Correction to the stage line: when the distance run and the route's progress are within 10% (and 0.5 km) it shows the distance run over the distance run in the whole stage (`46.4/46.9 km`), never actual over the route's length; only when they differ more does it show the route's progress over the route length with `ran X km` in red.

Markers on the overlay maps (`place_badges`, `tracks.overlay_places`): both maps mark the start (green disc with a play triangle), the checkpoints (blue discs with their number) and the finish (a chequered disc, the end of the last route), over the route lines and under the position arrow, which is always on top. When the start and the finish are closer than a badge (a loop) only the finish is drawn. The whole-route map places them once; the close-up map draws those in view (clear of the rounded edge).

Course facts for the script writer (`tracks.race_story`, `story_at`, `story_line`, `story_text`; `script_pack`): the race section of the prompt now has the course (stages, route and distance run in each, time), each checkpoint (when reached, how long stopped, its cut-off and the time to spare or late), the finish (reached or a DNF) and what the run covered of the routes; every clip (and gap) has a `race progress:` line for its middle moment: stage N of M, checkpoints done, route km into the stage, km to the next control, time left to its cut-off (or past it), how far ahead of or behind the last checkpoint's cut-off. The distance run is compared with the route covered only where they differ by more than 10% (and 0.5 km): then the line says it ran X km against Y km of route (detours or off course). Nothing about cut-offs is said until they are typed in.

Gap pages: every gap has a page like a clip, opened from the sidebar (under the clips) or the id in the Gaps panel: the preview of its generated clip with generate / regenerate / remove, how it is drawn (the planner chooses, 2D map or 3D flyover) and how long it is (the plan decides, exactly N seconds, or at least N seconds), must-use in the film, what the newest script draft says over it, and notes with the voice-over MUST INCLUDE (the same fields as a clip: the notes reach the script writer in the pack, and the narration lines become pins inside the gap). The choices are in synthetic.json (`settings`, `synthetic.set_settings`, `POST /api/gaps/settings`) and the planner keeps them: a kind you chose is used (`project.apply_gap_items`, `sync_gap_clips`), a set length is fixed (`script_plan.gap_choice`: the music fit never changes it, narration over it still gets its time), a minimum is never shortened below, and a must-use gap the script left out is added where it falls in the race (`force_gaps`).

Photos (`photos.py`, `/api/photos*`, `PhotosPanel`, `PhotoStrip`): photos taken during the race are added on the overview page (any format Pillow reads, and HEIC / HEIF, converted with pillow-heif or macOS `sips`; the pages always show JPEG copies: `thumb`, up to 4000 px wide). When: the EXIF GPS clock if there is one, else the camera's local time with its UTC offset, else the local time taken to be in the race's time zone (the page says which). Where: the photo's own GPS first, else where the run was at that time; both when it has both, and a warning when they are more than 500 m apart (a wrong camera clock, or taken away from the run), or when the time is outside the run. The time says which clip or gap it was taken in: it is listed on that clip's or gap's page and in the overview list, and has a camera icon at its place on the overview map (hover for the picture, click to open its clip or gap). A photo with no time stamp is refused with the reason. Removed photos are kept under photos/removed.

Photo analysis (`analysis/photo_analysis.py`, `strata360 photos-analyse`, `POST /api/photos/analyse`): the clip stages that make sense for a still photo are run over the uploaded photos, each redone only when its version, the photo or what it reads changes (or with force): `exposure` (luma, the share clipped, the spread in stops), `quality` (detail, blur, haze, contrast, saturation; featureless / blurry / hazy), `places` (the address and named places within a kilometre at the photo's position, with the race facts at that moment), `people` (YOLO pose persons and InsightFace faces with head pose and embeddings; `.venv-vision`, `analysis/photo_detect.py`), `identity` (which face is the wearer by the face profile, and how many people are in shot), `face_view` (is the wearer's face clear), `scenes` (the local vision model: setting, light, weather, tags, scenery 1-10 and clarity; one model load for all the photos) and `thumb_overlay` (the picture with the race overlay as at the time it was taken; none for a photo outside the run). The clip stages with nothing to work on in a still are ingest, the sound stages (extract, clean, background, events, levels, transcribe, align, transcript_check, speakers), motion, proxy, thumb / thumb_best and candidates. A stage that cannot run (no wearer profile, no models) fails loudly after the others have been tried. Results are in photos/analysis/<id>.json; the overview and the clip and gap pages show them on each photo, and the panel has Analyse photos / Redo all with the progress.

Pan and zoom on photos (`edit/photo_motion.py`, `/api/photos/motion*`, `PhotoMotion`): a move is a camera path over the photo, a 16:9 window whose centre and zoom change with a smooth ease, always inside the photo and never zoomed past `max_upscale` (1.5x) of its real pixels. It is aimed at what the photo analysis found, most important first: your face, other faces and persons, the objects (not tiny), the most detailed block of the picture (a saliency measure), else the thirds. Styles: push in (whole picture to the subject), pull out, pan across (the long way of a wide or tall photo, ending on the side the subject is on; a photo with no room is zoomed in a little first), drift (one point of interest to the next), reveal (a detail at high zoom, widening to the next) and hold (a slight breath); `auto` picks by the shape of the photo and what is in it, the seed choosing among the good ones and the direction when nothing decides (the same seed always gives the same move). Each photo card has Pan & zoom: the style, the length (2 to 30 s), another take, the start (green) and end (red) windows drawn on the picture, Watch (an MP4 made when first asked for and kept in photos/motion/) and Use this move, which keeps the choice in photos.json. Using the moves in the plan and the render of the film is the next step.

Objects (`analysis/photo_objects.py`, the photo stage `objects`): the everyday objects in a photo (YOLO11 on its 80 classes, people left to `people`) with boxes, shown on the card and used as pan and zoom targets; not run for clips.

Photos in the film (`script_pack.photo_clips`, `edit/photo_clip.py`, the `photo` script item): every photo is a pack clip `P1`... in time order with the clips and gaps, described in the prompt (when and where, the scene, place, scenery, who is in it, the objects, how it looks) and marked THE RUNNER WANTS THIS PHOTO IN THE FILM when its card says Use in the film (`photos.json` `must`). The writer may use one with a `photo` item (2.5 to 12 s; the editor pans and zooms on it), put a `vo` item over it, or leave it out, and a must-use photo with no item is a problem that sends the draft back. The plan treats a photo as a picture-only generated clip: `photo_clip.sync` renders its move (the photo's style and seed, aimed by the analysis) to a 4K video of exactly the whole-beat window the plan gives it (kept while the photo, the length and the move are unchanged), `synthetic.json` holds it as kind `photo` (t0 = t1 = when it was taken, so the overlay shows the race as it was then), and the preview and final film play it like a gap clip. A must-use photo the script leaves out is added where its time falls (`force_gaps`, with the gaps marked must), at the length of its move; removing a photo removes its clip.

Photo length and move (correction): a photo is shown for 2 to 3 s, not as long as a gap: `photo_analysis.auto_seconds` gives 2 s to a plain picture and 3 s to a busy one (`busyness`: the fine detail and how many people, faces and objects are in it), the plan fits it to the music between 2 and 3 s, and a length you set on the photo (2 to 8 s) is kept exactly. The pan and zoom depends on the length: a shot under 3.5 s only gets a push in, a pull out or a hold (a very wide or tall photo is still panned), and every move is made in proportion to its time (all of it at 6 s or more, a third of it at 2 s) so it is not too fast to watch.

**OpenStreetMap lookup service and road stretches.** `gps/osm.py` is a cached lookup service for the Overpass API: a pool of eight public mirrors used in round robin (a mirror at most once per 1.1 s, a failing mirror rests 20 s doubling to 10 min and is forgiven by its next success), parallel `query_many`, and every good answer kept on disk (a failure is never cached; cache files are named like the places stage's, so its existing answers still hit). The places stage now asks the pool when it uses the public server (a server set in race.json is still asked alone). `gps/roads.py` `stretches(lat, lon, dist, cache_dir)` returns the runs of a track (sampled every 20 m) that are within 8 m of a drivable road for at least 300 m, fetched per 0.1 degree tile. Tried on the Legends track: 36 stretches, 23.9 km, in about 160 s over a cold cache. This is the base for a street-view gap option (Mapillary token and Google Maps key are in `secrets.env`, not yet used by the app).

**Street view page.** `streetview.py` keeps in `<race_dir>/streetview/` one stage for the road parts of the run (`roads.json`, from `gps/roads.py` and the cached Overpass mirror pool) and one stage for each of three providers (`mapillary.json`, `panoramax.json`, `google.json`). A provider stage records the SECTIONS of imagery on the road parts: one per capture run (a Mapillary sequence, a Panoramax collection, a run of Google panoramas) along one stretch, split where the frames leave a gap over 60 m. A section is `360` (a panoramic camera, sees every way) or `2d` (a flat camera: each frame has its angle to the runner's way, counted as forward / right / back / left), with km range, length, picture count, spacing, years, camera and native size. Frames count only within 12 m of the run's line. A provider stage is redone when the roads change (it records the roads' id). `strata360 streetview F [--stages ...] [--force]` runs them; the server runs the same in the background (`/api/streetview`, `/run`, `/image`); the keys (`MAPILLARY_TOKEN`, `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` in secrets.env) never reach the browser. Mapillary and Panoramax pictures are kept in `streetview/img/` (CC BY-SA, credit them); Google's are fetched for display each time and never kept (its terms). The web page (under Overview in the sidebar) shows the stages with Run / Redo, a map with the road parts (click one) and a marker per section (provider colour, "360" or "2D"), the pictures of the chosen section (one per way a flat camera faced), filters, and the sections as a list. Next: offer a section to the director and as a gap style.

**Street view: choosing sections.** `streetview.annotate` gives every section a stable key (`provider:sequence:km`), whether it is plausible (at least 30 pictures, 150 m, pictures no more than 10 m apart; Google's are never offered, its terms), its play time at 15 pictures a second and the apparent speed, the ids of the sections over the same road (overlap of at least 30% of the shorter one: another source or date) and your choice. The page lists the plausible sections with details, three previews, the overlap warning and a choice: not used / possible / must include, saved in `streetview/choices.json` (`POST /api/streetview/choice`). Only chosen sections go on to the script writer and the plan (next).

**Street view in the film.** A section you mark possible or must include (page: Street view) gets a permanent label V1, V2 ... (`streetview/choices.json`). Only those go on: `script_pack.streetview_clips` makes each a pack clip (no footage, no words; where it is on the course, the camera, when it was filmed and whether that light fits the race passing there, the sections over the same road) and the prompt offers a `streetview` item whose length is between 2 s and the section's slowest smooth speed (4 pictures a second blended to 30 frames a second; there is no fastest: pictures are skipped, so any clip down to 2 s plays the whole stretch). `script_draft.check` holds the item to that range, requires the must-include ones and refuses two over the same road; `script_plan` places it like a photo (a generated picture-only piece, fitted to the music within its range, added in its place when marked must and left out); `project.plan_from_script` makes the clips with `edit/streetview_clip.sync` (kind `streetview`, covering the race minutes of the section, so the overlay runs through them). Credits name Mapillary / Panoramax (CC BY-SA); a photo clip no longer gets a map credit. The camera (`edit/streetview_cam.py`): Mapillary 360 uses the true rotation Mapillary reconstructed for each picture to level the horizon, heading along the smoothed path of the camera positions, looking 25 m ahead; Panoramax 360 estimates which way is up from the picture (about 3 degrees) and heads along the road; flat cameras use the pictures that face forward, steadied by matching the far field of neighbours and cropping in, and may still wobble (the page says so). The pictures between two real ones are made by moving both along the optical flow. Settings were chosen by measuring the far-field shift between the finished clip's frames (`jit.py` in the experiment folder): heading from the camera path (not the road line), smoothing 4 pictures, look-ahead 25 m for Mapillary; 1.5 pictures of smoothing for the estimated up and 5 for the heading for Panoramax. Not done: the daytime check is only a warning in the prompt, frame-to-frame matching did not help the 360 clips (too few matches between pictures 7 to 15 m apart), and different virtual cameras (glances toward objects exist in the prototype, not here).

**Street view quality score.** `edit/streetview_cam.py` `quality` measures how good a clip of a section will look without making it, and `streetview.run` stage `quality` does it for every plausible Mapillary / Panoramax section (on the smaller 2048 wide copies; a section already scored is kept, one whose pictures changed is scored again). Two measurements: the in-between PSNR (for sample pictures the middle one is rebuilt from its two neighbours by the flow blend and compared with the real one: this falls with picture spacing, bad levelling and things moving near the road) and the steadiness of a 4 s test clip at 25 m/s (the far field followed frame to frame: jerk and turning). The score is 0 to 100 (half in-between, a third jerk, the rest turning; thresholds `SCORE_*`) and graded good (65+), fair (40+) or poor. Measured on the three samples it ranked them the way a person did: Mapillary 360 78 (good), Panoramax 360 63 (fair), the flat dashcam section 9 (poor). The page shows the grade on each candidate with the measurements on hover; it does not block a poor one. A first version measured the shake between source pictures, which the forward motion itself inflates (44 degrees for the Panoramax section): measure on the frames of a clip played at a fixed road speed instead, so sections of different spacing compare fairly.

**Street view page, later additions.** A Filters menu (quality, steadiness, light, position among the footage, usable); for each section when it was filmed and when the runner passed it (race time zone), the nearest camera clip each way along the run with the distance and the time, the clips it overlaps and the gap it fills; the light warning from the sun's height at the real dates and place of the filming and of the pass (both heights in the message); a preview video made in the background, kept (named by the section and the camera version) and played on the page. **Clicking the map** asks Mapillary, Panoramax and Google for the nearest street view to that point as the crow flies, whatever the run did there (`streetview.near_point`: the nearest capture runs, searched 100, 250 then 500 m round; Google's nearest panoramas by asking at and round the point), each with the rules that would rule it out written out as they apply to a section: how far it is from the run's track, whether the run was on a road there, which way a flat camera faced, the pictures nearby against what a clip needs, the light, whether it overlaps camera footage. No filters apply (nothing about Google's terms: whether to use Google is your choice); the pictures are fetched only for what that search found.
