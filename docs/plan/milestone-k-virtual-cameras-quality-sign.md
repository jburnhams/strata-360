## Milestone K: virtual cameras, quality signals and the optimiser (design 1 Oct; feeds V1's preferred content and V5's cuts)

The idea: for each clip, a list of **virtual cameras** in order of quality. A camera has a start and an end time (it need not cover the clip) and is either a **tracking** or a **free** camera:
- **Trackings** (full clip length by default, shortened at either end where the picture is bad there; each with its own field-of-view rules): **heading** (where the runner goes), **you** (the wearer: the head and body rules we have), **person** (another person), and **scenery** (below). Heading and the others may favour an ultra-wide view and zoom in to avoid artefacts at the edge.
- **Free cameras**: a fixed pose and field of view, or a start pose and an end pose with a pan or zoom between them; far more flexible; about one per 15 s of clip, at most 12.
Then the film is chosen in two steps: first **which part of each clip** (transcript spans decide it where there is a transcript, extended if the film needs the time; otherwise the best-quality stretches of the best cameras, optimised against the time the script and music need), then **which camera at each moment** (cuts follow the music's energy; favour you or person where there is a transcript). This is an optimisation over several signals, with **seeded randomness** for variety (same seed, same film).

**Scenery tracking (added 1 Oct): a tracking view that avoids every person and takes the best-quality scenery available.**
- *Avoid people:* the view may not contain a person box (from `people.json`, inflated by a margin; the wearer and the pole count) above a small share of its area, and the rear direction where the wearer sits is a no-go unless the camera looks elsewhere.
- *Best quality:* a scenery-only score. Direction prior from the VLM's front and rear scenery scores (the prompt ignores people, since the people stage knows them), refined by the quality grid on the 15 degree cells (exposure; a heavy flat or foggy patch counts against a view only when it takes about half of it; detail only as a tie-break).
- *Movement, as for the other trackings:* either **slow and steady** (a rate limit; it moves only when another direction has been clearly better for a few seconds) or a **quick jump cut**, and **jump cuts are rationed** (a budget per minute, default 2; pans last at least a minimum time). One dynamic programme over yaw bins: pan cost grows with the angle, a jump costs a fixed amount, the budget is enforced, and a little seeded noise in the costs gives variety.
- Used for b-roll when there is no transcript (we do not favour people then) and for contrast with people shots (a people-free alternative the VLM cannot undo).

**Signals** (quality is always a **soft score**: nothing is marked unusable, as a lot of ultra footage is foggy; droplet detection was tried and dropped, see `progress.md`):
1. VLM, front and rear every 5 s: a graded 1 to 10 scenery score and a 1 to 5 clarity (scenes stage v2). Both good: the moment is usable in any direction; one much better: favour that direction; both bad: a negative score for b-roll (soft: a crowded start line may still matter for the story).
2. The quality grid from the proxy (`scripts/quality_spike.py`): exposure, flat share, detail, as guardrails and tie-breaks, never to rank (they punish open, beautiful landscapes).
3. People, identity and speakers (`people.json`, `identity.json`, `speakers.json`), the transcript spans and the alignment.
4. Motion: heading and gyro speed (blur).
5. The music: energy per bar sets shot length; beats place the cuts.

- **K6. Three views of you: mid, close, far (idea 3 Oct, from the user; to build).** The wearer's own view (the "you" subject, `framing.choose_subject` and the `selfie_hold` family: fov 85 in the body frame, yaw 180) gets two more cameras, so there are more shots to cut between: **you mid** (today's), **you close** (a face zoom, centred on the head, fov about 45 to 55, following the head) and **you far** (ultra wide, fov about 120 to 140, the whole body and the surroundings). The two new ones are **available for a clip only when the distance allows**: `views.focus_samples` gives the wearer's apparent height `h` (degrees) and head position per moment, which is the distance signal. *Far* needs the whole body to fit with a margin and clear of the stitching seam (h below about 55 degrees); *close* needs a found face that is not already filling the picture (a head box, h below about 70 degrees) and, at 4K, accepts a softer picture (the lens video is magnified a further 1.7 times: fine at 1080p and 2K, noted on the clip). Availability is a per-window share of time, computed with the other candidate features (`you_close_ok`, `you_far_ok`, 0 to 1; candidates stage version bump, 0.4 s a clip to redo) and a technique may require it (`techniques.json`: `selfie_close`, `selfie_far`, dialogue_ok like `selfie_hold`). They are in normal use, not special: the beam search picks them like any technique, and the multicam cutter (K5, V5) alternates mid, close and far inside a long talking window (a 15 s dialogue becomes three shots, cut on the beat or on a phrase gap) so a talking head is never one shot for 20 s. **The director:** the pack lists for each clip which of mid, close and far it has and for how much of its time (`you_views`), and an item may ask for one (`"view": "close"` on an emotional line, `"far"` to show the body and the surroundings); the plan uses it when available and says when not. **Built (3 Oct):** `candidates` stage 4 adds the features `you_close` and `you_far` (`candidates.you_view_arrays`: close needs you found and no taller than 72 degrees in the rear view, an unknown height is fine; far needs you found and a KNOWN height of 60 degrees or less; heights are interpolated across the clip from the identity samples that have one); techniques `selfie_close` (fov 50, `aim_face` centres the head) and `selfie_far` (fov 130, the body centred), both talking-head shots that need their feature (a view the footage does not have never fits); `chrono.assign_techniques` honours a window's `view` request (`VIEW_TECH`) and warns when it cannot; `script_plan` cuts a talking stretch of 9 s or more into shots of about 6 s in the pauses between lines when its footage has a close or far view (`split_points`, `dialogue_windows(cuts=)`), so the shots can differ; the pack carries `you_views` per clip (`script_pack.you_views`) and the writer can put `"view"` on clip and broll items (prompt 9, checked against what the clip has). On Legends the typical wearer height is about 50 degrees in the rear view, so far is available in most clips (mostly where heights were found) and close wherever you are found. Judged on real stills (`scripts/views_spike.py`, 3 Oct, clips 0018, 0021, 0024, 0008): far (130 degrees) shows the whole body and the surroundings well; close needed two fixes found by looking: the three views now look where you ARE (world frame; before, yaw 180 behind the runner missed you when you were off to one side, which also improved mid), the face is aimed at 30 percent of your height below the detection's top (7 percent put the hood in the centre), and the follower's dead band scales with the field of view (a close view must not let you drift out of frame). Not done: the seam test for far and resolution notes at 4K.
**Done when:** unit tests on synthetic focus samples (close and far only when the distance fits; none when you are not found; a seam crossing rules far out); the planner picks them in a plan from a real clip; a 15 s dialogue window is cut into more than one view; a pack and a draft carry `you_views` and `view`.

- **K7. Point cameras (idea 4 Oct, from the user; built, preview and film clip from the proxy).** A virtual camera that keeps ONE PLACE in frame while a clip or a street view section goes past it: you arm "Add a point camera" on the page of a clip or a street view section, click the map within 400 m of its path, and the camera looks at that point from wherever the path is, panning round and zooming with the distance. It is the first camera made by the user rather than found by the planner, and it is offered to the planner two ways (a camera the planner may cut to inside a clip, and a generated shot of its own, the way gaps, photos and street view sections are), so it has the same Not used / Possible / Must include selector, notes, voice-over card, film list entry and credits. Code: `edit/pointcam.py` (the records in `<race>/pointcams.json`, the geometry), `edit/pointcam_clip.py` (the source path, the shot, the preview, the film clip, `sync`), `streetview_cam.render_point`, `synthetic.make_pointcam` (kind `pointcam`, id C1, C2 ...), `script_pack.camera_clips`, the `camera` item of the script (prompt 14), `script_plan` (pieces, `flex`, `force_gaps`), `project.plan_from_script` (`pointcam_clip.sync`), `/api/pointcams*`, `strata360 pointcam-video`; web `PointCams.tsx` (`usePointCams`, `PointCamTool`, the card, the settings, the preview with progress and log), `PointCamPage.tsx`, `mapLayers.addPointCams`, the sidebar row.
  - *The camera.* The path is `dict(lat, lon, t)`: for a clip the race track during the clip (a few seconds either side to smooth from), for a street view section a fix at each picture, timed by where the runner was on the course. The position at a moment is the path smoothed over 1.5 s (a GPS jitter of 2 m is a big swing of the view when the point is 20 m away). The view looks at the compass bearing from the camera to the point, smoothed (`smooth_s`, default 0.8 s, on the unwrapped angle), at a pitch that aims at the height of the point (`height_m`, 0 for the ground; the camera is taken to be 2.2 m up), with the zoom between `fov_near` (95 degrees, at the closest approach) and `fov_far` (55 degrees, from twice that distance on): wide where the view swings fast, tight when far. Defaults come from the shape of a pass, nothing was fitted to footage.
  - *The stretch.* `before_m` and `after_m` (40 m each by default) of path round the closest approach, held to the clip or section and to 2 to 60 seconds; the page says how long that is, how near and far the point is, how far the view turns and its fastest pan, and warns when the pan is faster than 45 degrees a second (a point close to the path) or the path goes within 4 m of the point. A clip's shot plays at the speed it was filmed; when the film asks for fewer seconds the shot is trimmed on both sides round the closest approach (the point is always in it). A street view shot plays the whole window in the length asked for (its pictures are 5 to 15 m apart, so real time would be a slide show; the default is the road at 25 m/s), blending neighbouring pictures along the optical flow as the street view clip does.
  - *Frames.* A clip's yaw is in the proxy's upright world frame: the compass bearing of the point minus a calibration `north_offset` (the circular mean of the GPS course minus the clip's own smoothed heading, once a second over the clip, `analysis/views.heading_fn`), so the clip's frame need not be north-referenced. Checked with real ffmpeg on a synthetic proxy with a marker at a known direction (`tests/integration/test_pointcam_render.py`, headings 0 and 30 degrees): the marker is in the middle of the frame at the closest approach, and early on it is not. A street view shot is aimed by compass bearing through the rig's own levelled rotation (`Rig.rot`), so only 360 sections (Mapillary 360, Panoramax 360, Google as stitched panoramas, which are fetched on demand like the look-around video) can be aimed; a flat camera gives a clear error.
  - *The planner, two ways.* (1) **Cut in (clips, possible).** A camera on a clip marked Possible is a technique `cam:C1` in the planner's library for that run (`techniques.with_cams`, from `pointcam_clip.cutins`; family `pointcam`, 2 s up to 16 s or the stretch, not over speech, at most two uses). `chrono.assign_techniques` offers it only for a window that lies inside the stretch the camera covers (`cam_fits`, half a second of slack for whole-beat windows) and favours it (`Settings.w_cam`, 0.6 a second of window, like the other scores). B-roll of that clip is taken over the place where it can be (`script_plan.prefer_part`: the first window of an item goes inside the stretch, centred on the closest approach, when unused speech-free footage is there), so a possible camera usually gets a window to be chosen for; otherwise it is simply not used. The framing of such a window is the camera's path for its seconds (`framing.cam_path`, world frame, linear keyframes every 0.2 s), so the preview and the FINAL render draw it from the lenses like any other shot (the proxy-quality limit below is only for the generated clip). (2) **A shot of its own (any source, possible or must).** A pack clip `C1` and a `camera` item, rendered once for the plan's length (`pointcam_clip.sync`). The two are never both used for one camera: when the plan has the camera as an item (written by the writer, or added because it is must), its technique is banned for that plan; a must camera is therefore always its own shot, and a street view camera can only be that. Credits name a street view provider, not your own footage.
  - *Glides.* A cut between a camera shot and the next or previous shot of the same clip is a glide wherever `pans.plan` allows one: the camera's path is a world-frame path with no subject to keep in view, so only the move limits apply (at most 55 degrees of yaw, 20 of pitch, 85 of field of view, at most 80 degrees a second, 0.6 to 1.7 s, not more than 0.4 of either shot, and the middle must not look much worse than the ends). The planner hopes for a glide to or from a camera (`glide_pair` treats a camera as able to glide with any shot but itself) and `swap_for_glides` tests the real paths, so a cut that cannot glide is left as a hard cut or one shot is swapped for an equivalent technique. A camera shot is also on the Timeline's technique list (`/api/edit` includes the cameras), and a camera taken away after a plan was made is drawn as a plain wide shot.
  - *Seeing the aim.* Under the clip's player and the street view section's preview videos the page lists the cameras of that source; picking one draws, while the video plays inside the camera's stretch, a dot where it aims and the outline of its frame, with a Go to button for a clip. The outline is worked out each frame from the view the player has at that moment (`web/src/aimOverlay.ts`, the players' own projection), so it stays right while you drag the picture or an aim mode (heading, you, person, scenery) follows something. The server gives the camera's aim over time (`/api/pointcams/path`, `pointcam_clip.aim_path`): for a clip in the clip's own frame and seconds; for a street view look-around relative to the middle of each picture (`streetview_cam.headings` gives where that looks); for the section's flat preview relative to its fixed view (any 360 section: 85 degrees and a little down for Mapillary and Panoramax, the level 90 degree views asked of Google; a flat-camera section says it cannot be drawn on).
  - *Tried on real footage (4 Oct, Legends clip 0021 from the Expansion drive, a copy of the project's metadata in a scratch folder).* A point 20 m to the right of the run, the clip's frame is 95 degrees off north (`north_offset`), and the preview (37 s, 927 frames at 25 fps, 10 s to render) keeps the same mossy bank in view as the runner passes it. Not yet judged: tight zooms from the proxy, high pan rates, a point behind trees.
  - *Not done.* The generated clip of a footage shot (way 2) is rendered from the clip's PROXY (3840x1920 equirect, so a 55 degree view is about 600 pixels wide, enlarged to the film), not from the lenses; the cut-in way 1 does not have this limit. The generated clip carries no sound (picture only, like the other generated clips); a cut-in window plays the clip's own sound as every footage window does. Several cameras on one clip are allowed but two overlapping ones are not coordinated (the planner takes at most one per window). A moving point and a point behind the runner at the start or end of a clip are not offered. A must camera is not cut in (it is its own shot); a possible street view camera is only a generated clip.

**Clips whose camera dropped frames (3 Oct, clip 0021 of Legends, the main running clip):** its picture bounced against the other lens and the seam was visibly torn. Two separate causes, both only in clips whose timestamps jump (0021 jumps 40 ms at frame 20 and 180 ms at frame 28; 0004, 0013 and 0023 have none): (1) the video has undecodable frames after the jump (missing references); the **hardware decoder silently leaves them out** (28 of 3149 frames in one lens), so the two lens streams were paired one-for-one 28 frames out of step for the rest of the clip. Software decoding with the damaged frames kept (`-flags +output_corrupt`) gives one frame per packet and the lenses agree (seen on the tree trunks across the seam); `flat.decode_args` chooses it for such a clip (`telemetry.has_dropped_frames`), and the proxy now **refuses to finish** if the lens streams do not give the expected number of frames. (2) The final render found the frame at clip time t as t x 50; after a 180 ms jump that is a frame 9 too early, so picture and orientation row were 9 frames apart; `flat.frame_at` finds it by the frame timestamps. The orientation rows themselves are one per frame and in step with the pictures (an earlier commit that shifted them by the frames lost was wrong, and is removed: my own lag test had made the same nominal-time mistake). **Proxies made before this have the lenses out of step for such a clip: 0021's must be rendered again.** The motion analysis of 0021 is not affected by the decoders (it reads the orientation rows only).

The close view aims at it (eyes and nose in the middle, 0.8 degrees off on 0021 against 9 before); the mid and far views add only its FAST motion (weights 0.7 and 0.5), on top of the calm aim. The pipeline's own detections (box centre, stored face centre) are too inexact for this. **`face_view` stage** (`analysis/face_view.py`, off by default like `people`, needs `.venv-vision`): once a second the head pose of the wearer's face (insightface 3D landmarks, pitch and yaw as the crop sees it) says whether the face is CLEAR (found, pitch not below -50, yaw within 45); checked against marked moments of 0023 (good 0:10, 0:33, 2:40; bad 0:34 profile, 0:35 and 2:41 looking down) and 0021 (mostly good). Ears and eye/nose confidence were tried first and do not work (a hat or hood hides the ears; the model finds eyes and nose on a face looking down). **You close** is allowed only where the face is clear in 60% of its seconds (when the clip has been analysed), in busy footage for at most 3 s (up to 8 s in calm footage: `chrono.calm` from the candidate's `steady`), and only next to a mid view in the same clip to glide in from or out to (`chrono.assign_techniques`, enforced after the search too; a requested close that cannot be given becomes the mid view, with a warning). Dialogue is cut into shorter shots the busier the footage (target 3 to 6 s), in busy footage also inside speech (the picture cuts, the clip's sound runs on, `split_points(free=True)`, `join_runs`). Run `strata360 run FOLDER --stages face_view` to analyse the clips (about a minute per clip).

**The clip's voice only over the wanted lines (3 Oct):** a dialogue window can run past the lines the script wants (rounded up to whole beats, or lengthened), and the clean audio there would play the words of the next sentence. Each dialogue window now records `voice_span` (the part of the window that is the wanted lines, `script_plan.build`), and `preview.build_audio` plays the clip's voice only over it (muted elsewhere, plus the never-say mutes) and the speech-free background track (`audio_background.flac`) everywhere else in the window; the rough mix and the final film use the same builder. Plans made before this have no `voice_span` and play as before (re-plan). 

**Tracking you in the tight views (3 Oct, findings):** `framing.you_track` follows you at 25 Hz through the camera's own motion, which cut the wander from the old follower about four-fold, but it cannot do better than its input: the pipeline's detections of you are one a second and inexact (the box centre moves with your arms; the stored face centre is on the face in only about a third of six consecutive frames on 0021), so the direction to you in the camera's frame varies by about 10 degrees from one detection to the next and cannot be predicted from neighbours (held out: 11 degrees whatever the smoothing). A frame-by-frame feature tracker between detections (`analysis/subject_track.py`, opt-in with `STRATA_YOU_KLT=1`) did not predict held-out detections better than a straight line, so it is off. **The real fix needed the head found directly at the frame rate** (YOLO pose keypoints on stabilised crops): that was built the same day (next paragraph, `analysis/head_track.py`), so this paragraph is the finding that led to it.

**Footage is never shown twice (3 Oct):** its sound would be heard twice. `script_plan.take` no longer has a "reused" tier: when a clip has less unused footage than a b-roll asks for, the window is shorter (warning "only N s of unused footage ... shortened"), and with none left (under the shortest window) the piece is left out. Before the film is fitted to the music `cap_broll` asks each b-roll for no more than its clip has unused (after the dialogue the script plays and the pieces before it) and passes that as the longest it may be stretched to; after the fit, gap clips are added for the time still missing (`auto_gaps`) and then, if the film is still more than a bar short of the music, b-roll from clips the script does not use, best footage first, in their place in the race (`auto_broll`, unused footage only).

**You views are tracked at the frame rate (3 Oct):** the detections are about once a second, which cannot follow a runner whose body swings with the camera. `framing.you_track` turns each detection into the camera's body frame (with the orientation of that moment, `views.stab_fn`, 50 Hz), smooths those directions in time with a window that follows the motion (3 s where you are steady in the body frame, down to 0.6 s where the detections around a sample disagree by 4 degrees or more), and turns the smoothed direction back into the world with the orientation of every frame, at 25 Hz keyframes (`TRACK_STEP_S`), with the head-room / face-centre offset added and a 0.12 s smoothing. Measured on 0021 (what is left once the steady offset is taken off): the subject wandered about 6 to 7 degrees in yaw and 4 to 6 in pitch with the old hold-and-pan follower, and 1 to 2.6 and 1.6 to 2.4 now. `STRATA_YOU_TRACK=0` goes back to the old follower. Applies to the three You views only; person and the other subjects are unchanged.

**You close / mid (3 Oct):** the close view aims at the centre of the face from the identity face box (`views.face_offsets`, `face` and `face_dyaw` in the you samples, focus.json schema 6), falling back to the head-top estimate, and is 58 degrees (was 50). The planner leans to the mid view (`Settings.tech_bias`), and the close view is only for dialogue windows (or when the script asks for it), at most 3 uses and 6% of the film. **Proxies:** the people-aware seam is now in the proxy renderer too (`proxy.clip_boxes`, `seam_people`; no stage version changed, so nothing is invalidated); proxies pick it up when they are next rendered, and the first proxy of a clip, made before the people are found, has nobody to steer round.

**Gap clips: the planner chooses 2D or 3D (3 Oct):** the script's `gap` item gives only the length (prompt v12, no `kind`); `synthetic.choose_kind` decides how it is drawn: a 3D flyover where the land is the story (ascent plus descent of 900 m or more), a 2D map for a short clip (under 5 s), a short hop (under 10 km) or flat ground (under 300 m of relief), and in between it alternates with the previous choice so the film has both. A clip already rendered, or one made by hand in the Gaps panel (`by: user`), keeps its kind. No approval step for flyovers. The 2D map clip draws the whole route that is on screen thin and grey under the stretch (red behind the marker, white ahead), so the clip reads as a part of the journey.

**Pan transitions (3 Oct):** `edit/pans.py` turns a plain cut between two consecutive windows of the SAME clip into a glide (pan and zoom between the two framings, 0.6 to 1.2 s) when it is gentle (at most 55 degrees of yaw, 20 of pitch, 85 of field of view, 80 deg/s; a zoom-heavy glide takes up to 1.7 s) and keeps the first shot's subject (you, a person) in view for the whole glide and the second's from the half way point; otherwise the hard cut stays. `STRATA_NO_PANS=1` turns it off. Rendered in `render/film.py` (`pan_extras`, piece kind `pan`; both halves use a per-frame `pose_extra` of degrees yaw, pitch, fov added to the path). `free_view` paths may also pan between two poses (`framing.free_path`). Globe (little planet) shots glide too: the view stays a globe all through (radius 1 / z, z the share of the view shown) while the camera lifts from straight down to the flat shot's pitch (`globe_plan`, a 4th `pose_extra` column holds the globe radius). Every glide is judged on the views it passes through (`pans.judge`, `looker`: `view_quality.view_score` at 7 samples; refused when the middle averages under 0.6 of the worse end or any sample under 0.3 of it, i.e. a glide through mostly sky or flat ground); the glide's `look` score is kept in the transition for choosing among candidates. Talking stretches cut into shots are snapped to whole beats inside a pause (`snap_cuts`) and the leftover overlap closed up to the pause (`join_runs`), so the shots form one continuous run. The planner now prefers glideable neighbours: `chrono.assign_techniques` adds `w_glide` (0.3) when two back-to-back windows of one clip get techniques that `pans.glide_pair` says can glide (a guess from the techniques' field of view and frame; the real test stays `pans.plan` on the framing), taking Legends from 3 to 5 glides of 12 same-clip cuts. After planning, `pans.swap_for_glides` (called from `project.plan_from_script`) swaps one shot of a pair that cannot glide for a broadly equivalent technique (planner score at least 85% of the chosen one's, no new hero shot) that can, tested on the real framing and view quality; the user's forced techniques and locked windows are never swapped. Legends: 5 to 7 glides of 12. The 5 left are 4 cuts between different script items (the footage jumps) and one heading-follow camera. Known limit (before that): dialogue splits overlap by 0.25 to 0.5 s from beat rounding, so only some same-clip cuts qualify (2 of 12 on Legends).
