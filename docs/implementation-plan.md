## Milestone V: the voice-over cut (first cut; critical path after A1)

The new order of operations, replacing plan, then script, then squeeze:

```
rough plan (clip blocks)  ->  script per block  ->  voice-over spoken / recorded and measured
      ->  re-size blocks to the measured voice-over  ->  windows inside blocks  ->  cuts snapped to the music's beats  ->  final audio placement
```

Terms: a **block** is one clip's continuous part of the film (the film stays chronological, so blocks are in shooting order); a block holds one or more **windows** (the existing plan segments: one view and technique each). Narration **lines** belong to blocks. A clip's **dialogue** (the wearer speaking on camera, the `speech` windows) is played in a gap between lines.

### V1. Rough plan: clip blocks and preferred content (M)
- `edit/chrono.py` step 1 (`allocate`) already splits the film between clips; expose it as a planning level of its own, in seconds rather than beats: per clip a block with a target length (the film target, D8: the music's length when a track is chosen, else the target length when one is given, else automatic, below), the usable stretches it may use, its **preferred content** (best candidates by kind and priority: `speech`, `you`, `person`, `scene`, `best`) and any dialogue it must carry (with its exact speech span from `alignment.json`, padded 60 ms before and 120 ms after, overview 16.4).
- **Which clips (D9):** a clip with no usable footage is dropped (and listed); every other clip gets a block of at least its minimum b-roll (one minimum window, 2 s, plus its dialogue if it has any). Only if the target cannot hold all the minimums are more clips dropped, lowest value first, each reported with the reason. This replaces today's "forced" unusable stretch in `edit/chrono.py`.
- **Automatic length (D8, no music and no target):** each clip's block gets a natural length from its usable footage with diminishing returns (for example 2 s + 1.5 x sqrt(usable seconds), capped at its usable footage) plus its dialogue; the sum is the first guide for the script, and the script's measured length then sets the film (V4).
- Locks, bans and per-clip weights (the existing overrides) apply at this level unchanged.
- **Music upload (1 Oct, first step of wiring V1 in):** the Overview has a Music panel (upload, or player + waveform + spectrogram + bar lines + energy and a "change" link). `<race dir>/music.json` holds the file name as uploaded, the analysis (bpm, first downbeat, sections, confidence), the waveform peaks and the spectrogram file (`edit/music.py` `store`/`info`; `/api/music`, `/api/music/audio`, `/api/music/spectrogram`). The track's length is what D8 will hand to `plan_blocks` as the film target; nothing reads it that way yet.
- **Built (1 Oct):** `edit/blocks.py` (`plan_blocks`), `tests/unit/test_blocks.py`; `project.load_clips` also passes `alignment`. Not yet wired into `project.propose` or the GUI (V2 consumes it). The film target is passed in; choosing it from the music or a target length is left to the caller.
- **Wired in as a command (1 Oct):** `strata360 plan-blocks FOLDER [--target-s N] [--auto] [--json]` (`project.rough_blocks`, writes `<race dir>/blocks.json`; length guide: `--target-s`, else the music track from its first downbeat, else automatic; the saved beat plan is untouched). **First run on Legends with a 247 s track found a problem:** every `speech` candidate is treated as dialogue the block must carry in full, which on Legends is 228 s of speech in 24 clips (27% of the usable footage) and block minimums of 276 s, more than the whole film, so 14 clips were dropped. ### V2b. Whole-race script writer with pins (decided 1 Oct; replaces "carry all the dialogue" in V1)
The writer sees every clip at once and decides what the film says: which of the runner's lines to keep, the narration that links them, and the picture each clip gets.
- **Input (`edit/script_pack.py`):** per clip, in shooting order: the clip number, local time, GPS distance, pace, place, what the scene model saw, the runner's note and the corrected transcript as numbered LINES with exact times; plus the race facts and the user's notes. About 7,600 tokens for Legends (25 clips, 1,637 words of speech). Film details now hold the **official race distance** (`distance_km`, entered in the Film details panel): the GPS length reads longer (367 km against a 350 km race) because of detours.
- **Length guide (D7, D8):** the music's length from its first downbeat; else a target; else automatic: about 5 minutes, at least 50% of the total clip length and at most max(50%, 10 minutes) when the quality of the clips and the amount of transcribed speech justify it. **Narration speed:** the average of the normal voice-over speed (145 wpm) and the runner's own speech rate (228 wpm on Legends), at most 1.25 times the voice-over speed (about 181).
- **Output:** JSON items played one after another: `clip` (the runner's own lines, `from` and `to` line ids), `vo` (narration, with a short `basis`), `broll` (picture, seconds); plus `skipped` clips with reasons. Durations are recomputed by the checker (`scripts/script_exp/run.py check`), never taken from the model; a retry sends the problems back.
- **Iterating (the point of the design):** the first reply is a draft. The user then marks transcript lines **MUST INCLUDE** or **DO NOT USE**, and writes or keeps narration with an anchor: **inside a clip's run**, **in order but anywhere**, or **anywhere** (`edit/script_pins.py`; the tags go into the clip list and a constraints section tells the writer how much time the fixed parts already use). The writer then **revises the current draft**, keeping every item the new constraints do not touch. Fixed parts make the problem smaller: they fix how long and how many clips are left.
- **Checks:** structure drives retries (length within 3%, shooting order, one run per clip, no more picture than a clip has usable, every pin honoured). **Grounding is advisory** (`edit/script_ground.py`): a narration `basis` that is not in the material, a number that is nowhere in it, a claim that rests on a transcript line which is not placed next to it. They are shown to the user as warnings, never block a draft.
- **Prompt rules that matter (all generic, nothing about one race):** narration 20 to 30 percent of the film in short bridging pieces; give the clips with no speech a reason to be there; never invent; put each fact on the clip it belongs to; when the runner's words show a claim, put those words right next to the narration; the ending gets room and mostly the runner's own voice; the runner's recordings can describe earlier events.
- **Experiment (Gemini 3.1 Pro, paid key, 1 Oct):** one call takes 60 to 150 s with 14 to 20k tokens in and about 2.5k out (roughly 5 to 10 cents). A first draft usually lands within 3% of the target on the first or second attempt. A pinned revision satisfied every pin on the first attempt. Narration share rises from 13% to about 29% with the "20 to 30 percent" rule. Hard grounding rules cut the invented facts but also cut good narration, so v4 asks for a `basis` and only warns. Two of my early "errors" were my own: a checker that used the GPS length as the race distance, and a track pack line that made "80 hours" read as a time limit.
- **To do:** the GUI (read the draft item by item; toggle lines MUST INCLUDE or DO NOT USE; add and anchor narration; "revise" with a diff; warnings beside the items); store the script and the pins in the project; then the script's clip items become V1's must-carry dialogue and per-clip seconds (V1 stops allocating from the speech candidates), V3 measures the narration, V4 fits it. Chronology note: the music-first target then comes from the script's length.

### V2c. Marking and editing the script in the GUI (design 2 Oct)
**One colour language, two places.** Every word of the transcript, and every word of the draft script, has a state the user can set by selecting text (dragging across words, lines and clips is allowed) and pressing a toggle (three buttons; keys G, R, W):
- **red = NEVER USE / never say**, **green = MUST USE / must say**, **white = don't care**.
- **yellow is not stored**: it is a transcript word that is white but used by the current draft (so white = could use but isn't, yellow = could use and is). It is worked out from the draft's clip items.
Where the states live:
- *Transcript:* `transcript_marks.json` beside `transcript_edits.json`, per word, with the same `segment:word` keys, so marks survive re-runs the way corrections do. The marks reach the writer by **splitting the lines at every change of state** (`0023.27` becomes `0023.27.1`, `.2`, ...), so the writer still only chooses whole lines and `script_pins` only needs line ids; word-level cuts come from the split.
- *Narration (the script's own words):* a green selection becomes a pin (the selected text, word for word, anchored to the clip it sits in; the user can loosen it to "in order" or "anywhere"); a red selection becomes a "never say" phrase that the checker looks for. Pins are stored as text, not offsets, so they survive a rewrite. **A manual edit of any narration word turns the edited words green.**
- *The transcript inside the script:* the clip items in the script view show the same words with the same colours, and a toggle or an edit there is the same operation as in the transcript panel: it writes `transcript_marks.json` or `transcript_edits.json` (an edited word is also marked green), as if done in situ.
**Notes get a narration field.** Next to the notes at both levels (the whole folder, and each clip) a "Voice-over MUST INCLUDE" text area, one piece of narration per line: at clip level each line is anchored inside that clip's run; at folder level each line is "anywhere", with a "keep in the order written" switch.
**Clash to avoid:** the transcript panel already uses amber and blue backgrounds for corrected words. The four colours above take the background; corrections move to a small marker (a dotted underline: amber for Gemini, blue for the user; the hover text stays).
**Open questions (defaults in brackets):** does red also silence that speech in the film's own sound wherever a window of that clip plays (yes: the sound ducks out, the picture stays)? Do green words force a skipped clip back into the film (yes)? Is the draft kept with each save so a revise can show a diff (yes, last three)?
- **Build order:** (1) `transcript_marks` and the narration fields in the notes (backend, tests); (2) split lines in `script_pack`, pins from marks and notes in `script_pins`; (3) `strata360 script-draft` and `/api/script2` (draft, revise, warnings); (4) the transcript panel's selection and toggle; (5) the script panel with the same selection, edits to pins, the diff and the warnings.

### V3. Measure the spoken voice-over (M)
- `edit/voiceover.py` stops squeezing: each line is spoken (Kokoro) or recorded at its natural length (no tempo change, no cut), and its real duration is measured; for recordings, leading and trailing silence are trimmed by energy and the words are force-aligned to the line's text with the existing aligner (`audio/align.py`) to get word times and to catch a missing or repeated line.
- Output `voiceover/vo.json` (overview 17.2): per line, take, natural duration, speech start and end inside the file, word times, alignment score, loudness. Recording a new take re-measures only that line.
- **Built (1 Oct):** `edit/vo_measure.py`: `measure_take` (one file per line), `measure_recording` (one recording of several lines, split by aligning all words at once, with the pauses between), `measure_script` (speaks or takes the recording of each line of a block script at natural length, writes `voiceover/vo.json`, reuses unchanged lines so a new take re-measures only its line). Line statuses: `ok`, `short` (words missing), `long` (extra or repeated), `mismatch`, `silent`, `missing`. The aligner is injected (default wav2vec2). Tests: `tests/unit/test_vo_measure.py` (tone "speech" and scripted aligners), one integration test with the fake engine. The old squeeze path in `voiceover.build` stays until V4/V6 replace it. **Not yet checked:** the real aligner and Kokoro on a real recording (P5-33 by hand: loudness is RMS dBFS, not LUFS; the status limits assume about 150 wpm).
- **Script path, built (2 Oct):** `voiceover.speech_span` trims every take (recorded or spoken) to its speech by energy: a line is as long as its speech, starts where its first word does, and `vo.json`-style timings carry `trim`. `line_durations` (what the planner sizes windows by) uses the trimmed length. Not yet: the aligner on real recordings.
- **Done when:** P5-33 on a synthetic recording (Kokoro reading a known text with inserted silences): line boundaries within 50 ms, a missing line reported.

### V4. Re-size blocks to the voice-over (M)
- New `edit/vo_fit.py`: each block's length = its lines' measured durations + the pauses between lines (default 0.4 s, after a paragraph 0.9 s) + its dialogue (padded) + lead-in and lead-out (default 0.5 s each, so a line never starts on a cut), never less than the block's minimum and never more than its usable footage (if it would be, the overflow is reported: "line 7 needs 9.2 s; clip 0012 has 6.5 s usable", with the choices to shorten the line, borrow from the next block, or allow a hold on the last frame).
- **Film length (D7).** The sum of the blocks is the voice-over timeline V; the music's length is M (from its first downbeat, or from where the user starts it). Hard rule: L >= V. Target: L = M. Settings (defaults to confirm): `fade_max_s` (how much of the music may be lost to an early fade-out, 8 s), `lead_silence_max_s` (silence before the music starts, 4 s), `tail_silence_max_s` (silence after it ends, 6 s).
  - **Music longer (M > V):** the extra M - V is spread over the blocks: longer holds and pauses inside each block's usable footage, extra windows from preferred content, and a music-only intro and outro (picture with no narration, up to a few bars each). If the footage cannot fill all of it, L = V + what can be filled and the music fades out over its last bar or at a section end; if more than `fade_max_s` of the music would be lost, the shortfall is reported (with which clips ran out of footage).
  - **Voice-over longer (M < V):** L = V. The music is placed inside the film with silence (or the clips' own sound) before and/or after it, split between start and end as the sync allows, each within its limit; beyond the limits the shortfall is reported with the options to pick a longer track, loop a section of the track at a bar line, or shorten the script (C2's per-block re-write, which this pulls forward). The script writer (V2) is given the music's length as its target, so this is the exception.
  - **Music position is free within the flexibility.** The music's start in the film (an offset, possibly negative: starting part-way into the track) is chosen in V5 together with the cuts, so downbeats fall on the cuts that matter (block boundaries, the first dialogue, the last cut); the film may grow by a beat or two for this, never shrink below V.
  - **No music:** L = V, plus a short lead-in and lead-out; with a target length the extra or missing time is handled as for music (spread over the blocks, or reported), but more loosely, since there is no beat to land.
- Lines get provisional start times in film time.
- **Speed as a last resort (decided 1 Oct):** before a block's overflow is reported, its **synthetic** lines are played faster, one factor for the block, at most 1.25x as before (recordings are never changed). The line is spoken again at `rate x tempo` words a minute, not time-stretched, and measured; its `vo.json` entry gets `tempo_applied`, which counts against the limit on the next fit.
- **Built (1 Oct):** `edit/vo_fit.py`: `fit` (blocks, lines, film length, music placement, problems with choices), `respeak`, `fit_project` (measure, fit, re-speak the sped lines, fit again). Tests: `tests/unit/test_vo_fit.py`, one integration test with a voice whose pace follows the rate. Not yet wired into `strata360 script`, the project file or the app. The music-only intro and outro are folded into the first and last block's lead-in and lead-out; V5 cuts the real windows. - **Script path, built (2 Oct):** a line that does not fit its room is flagged `over` with `choices` (shorten, more footage, hold the last frame, speak faster for the synthetic voice or record again); the script plan holds the last frame of a clip when its footage is shorter than the narration and warns with the same choices.

### V5. Windows inside blocks, then cuts on the beat (L, the heart of it)
- **Windows.** Inside each block, cut windows from the preferred content as `cut_windows` does now (non-overlapping, 2 to 8 s, techniques by the existing beam search), but in seconds, not beats.
- **Beat times, precisely.** `edit/music.py` gains a beat tracker that returns the actual time of every beat and downbeat (dynamic programming over the onset envelope, starting from today's constant-tempo estimate, allowed to drift), refined to the onset peak, so a beat is known to within a few milliseconds even where the tempo wanders. `music.json` stores the beat list.
- **Music offset.** Before snapping, choose the music's start in the film (within D7's limits) that puts the most important cuts (block boundaries, dialogue starts, the last cut) nearest to downbeats; a small search over offsets in steps of a few milliseconds, scored by the total snap distance.
- **Snapping.** Every cut (between windows and between blocks) moves to a nearby beat, preferring downbeats and bar lines, within a tolerance (default: half a beat either way). Hard rules:
  1. a dialogue window always contains its whole speech span plus padding: a cut next to speech may only move **outwards** (earlier before it, later after it), never into it;
  2. a cut never falls inside a voice-over word, and a line keeps its lead-in (a cut under a line between its words is fine);
  3. each window stays inside its technique's duration range and its clip's usable footage; snapping may lengthen the film by a beat or two but never makes it shorter than the voice-over (D7); lines are re-timed with their block.
  If no beat satisfies the rules, the cut stays at the nearest safe point off the beat and is reported in the plan ("cut 14 is 120 ms off the beat: the dialogue in clip 0023 ends there").
- **Milliseconds to frames.** The cut time is kept exact in the plan; the picture cuts on the nearest frame (within 10 ms at 50 fps, 8 ms at 60), the sound at the exact sample.
- **Without music:** the same pass with no beat list, so cuts land on the voice-over's pauses and the safe cut points only.
- **Done when:** unit tests on a synthetic beat list with a drifting tempo and a fixture with dialogue: every cut not reported as off-beat is within one frame of a beat; no dialogue span is shortened by a single sample; no cut inside a voice-over word; the reported off-beat cuts are exactly those the rules force; the same seed gives the same plan.
- **Built (7 Oct): `edit/beat_sync.py`, called from `project.plan_from_script` after the generated clips are placed (before glides and transitions).** The beats are the beat tracker's (G0a, `music_build.grid`, kept in `music/grid.json`; a built track's from the bar lines in `built.json`). A cut moves on its own: the shot after it starts that much earlier or later in its clip, so every other frame, the clips' sound and every narration line keep their film times and nothing has to be re-timed. The music's start is searched within half a beat in 5 ms steps (this also absorbs the analysis' ~23 ms early `offset_s`); a start before the track's beginning is a `delay_s` (`render/preview.build_audio` delays the music). Then one dynamic programme over the beats near each cut (bar lines preferred at block boundaries and the end, moving cut cost) with the rules: the runner's words (`voice_span`) stay whole (except between shots of one run of a clip, whose sound plays through), a narration line keeps its 0.12 s lead-in, no footage shown twice, inside the clip, a shot no further from its technique's length, a generated clip at most 8% faster or slower, the film not ending before the last word. The plan gains `beat_sync` (seek, shift, delay, counts, `off_beat` with the reason) and `film.music` the chosen `offset_s` / `delay_s`; a cut off the beat is also a warning ("cut 14 (118.8 s) is 24 ms off the beat: the words in clip 0021 end there"). Tests: `tests/unit/test_beat_sync.py`, an integration test with a click track (`test_script_plan_project.py`) and the delayed music (`test_roughmix.py`). **Dry run on the saved Legends plan (not saved):** music start 42 ms later than the analysis said; 43 cuts, all on a beat (9 on bar lines), 3 after the music ends, largest move 140 ms. **Not yet:** voice-over word times (a cut under a line is allowed anywhere between its first and last word); the old beat planner (`propose`) is not synced; cuts without music stay where they are.

## Milestone K: virtual cameras, quality signals and the optimiser (design 1 Oct; feeds V1's preferred content and V5's cuts)

The idea: for each clip, a list of **virtual cameras** in order of quality. A camera has a start and an end time (it need not cover the clip) and is either a **tracking** or a **free** camera:
- **Trackings** (full clip length by default, shortened at either end where the picture is bad there; each with its own field-of-view rules): **heading** (where the runner goes), **you** (the wearer: the head and body rules we have), **person** (another person), and **scenery** (below). Heading and the others may favour an ultra-wide view and zoom in to avoid artefacts at the edge.
- **Free cameras**: a fixed pose and field of view, or a start pose and an end pose with a pan or zoom between them; far more flexible; about one per 15 s of clip, at most 12.
Then the film is chosen in two steps: first **which part of each clip** (transcript spans decide it where there is a transcript, extended if the film needs the time; otherwise the best-quality stretches of the best cameras, optimised against the time the script and music need), then **which camera at each moment** (cuts follow the music's energy; favour you or person where there is a transcript). This is an optimisation over several signals, with **seeded randomness** for variety (same seed, same film).

**Scenery tracking (added 1 Oct): a tracking view that avoids every person and takes the best-quality scenery available.**
- *Avoid people:* the view may not contain a person box (from `people.json`, inflated by a margin; the wearer and the pole count) above a small share of its area, and the rear direction where the wearer sits is a no-go unless the camera looks elsewhere.
- *Best quality:* a scenery-only score. Direction prior from the VLM's front and rear scenery scores (the prompt ignores people, since the people stage knows them), refined by the quality grid on the 15 degree cells (exposure; a heavy flat or foggy patch counts against a view only when it takes about half of it; detail only as a tie-break).
- *Movement, as for the other trackings:* either **slow and steady** (a rate limit; it moves only when another direction has been clearly better for a few seconds) or a **quick jump cut**, and **jump cuts are rationed** (a budget per minute, default 2; pans last at least a minimum time). One dynamic programme over yaw bins: pan cost grows with the angle, a jump costs a fixed amount, the budget is enforced, and a little seeded noise in the costs gives variety.
- Used for b-roll when there is no transcript (we do not favour people then) and for contrast with people shots (a people-free alternative the VLM cannot undo).

**Signals** (quality is always a **soft score**: nothing is marked unusable, as a lot of ultra footage is foggy; droplet detection was tried and dropped, see `progress.md`):
1. VLM, front and rear every 5 s: a graded 1 to 10 scenery score and a 1 to 5 clarity (scenes stage v2). Both good: the moment is usable in any direction; one much better: favour that direction; both bad: a negative score for b-roll (soft: a crowded start line may still matter for the story).
2. The quality grid from the proxy (`scripts/quality_spike.py`): exposure, flat share, detail, as guardrails and tie-breaks, never to rank (they punish open, beautiful landscapes).
3. People, identity and speakers (`people.json`, `identity.json`, `speakers.json`), the transcript spans and the alignment.
4. Motion: heading and gyro speed (blur).
5. The music: energy per bar sets shot length; beats place the cuts.

The view looks at the compass bearing from the camera to the point, smoothed (`smooth_s`, default 0.8 s, on the unwrapped angle), at a pitch that aims at the height of the point (`height_m`, 0 for the ground; the camera is taken to be 2.2 m up), with the zoom between `fov_near` (95 degrees, at the closest approach) and `fov_far` (55 degrees, from twice that distance on): wide where the view swings fast, tight when far. A clip already rendered, or one made by hand in the Gaps panel (`by: user`), keeps its kind. No approval step for flyovers. The 2D map clip draws the whole route that is on screen thin and grey under the stretch (red behind the marker, white ahead), so the clip reads as a part of the journey.

**Pan transitions (3 Oct):** `edit/pans.py` turns a plain cut between two consecutive windows of the SAME clip into a glide (pan and zoom between the two framings, 0.6 to 1.2 s) when it is gentle (at most 55 degrees of yaw, 20 of pitch, 85 of field of view, 80 deg/s; a zoom-heavy glide takes up to 1.7 s) and keeps the first shot's subject (you, a person) in view for the whole glide and the second's from the half way point; otherwise the hard cut stays. `STRATA_NO_PANS=1` turns it off. Rendered in `render/film.py` (`pan_extras`, piece kind `pan`; both halves use a per-frame `pose_extra` of degrees yaw, pitch, fov added to the path). `free_view` paths may also pan between two poses (`framing.free_path`). Globe (little planet) shots glide too: the view stays a globe all through (radius 1 / z, z the share of the view shown) while the camera lifts from straight down to the flat shot's pitch (`globe_plan`, a 4th `pose_extra` column holds the globe radius). Every glide is judged on the views it passes through (`pans.judge`, `looker`: `view_quality.view_score` at 7 samples; refused when the middle averages under 0.6 of the worse end or any sample under 0.3 of it, i.e. a glide through mostly sky or flat ground); the glide's `look` score is kept in the transition for choosing among candidates. Talking stretches cut into shots are snapped to whole beats inside a pause (`snap_cuts`) and the leftover overlap closed up to the pause (`join_runs`), so the shots form one continuous run. The planner now prefers glideable neighbours: `chrono.assign_techniques` adds `w_glide` (0.3) when two back-to-back windows of one clip get techniques that `pans.glide_pair` says can glide (a guess from the techniques' field of view and frame; the real test stays `pans.plan` on the framing), taking Legends from 3 to 5 glides of 12 same-clip cuts. After planning, `pans.swap_for_glides` (called from `project.plan_from_script`) swaps one shot of a pair that cannot glide for a broadly equivalent technique (planner score at least 85% of the chosen one's, no new hero shot) that can, tested on the real framing and view quality; the user's forced techniques and locked windows are never swapped. Legends: 5 to 7 glides of 12. The 5 left are 4 cuts between different script items (the footage jumps) and one heading-follow camera. Known limit (before that): dialogue splits overlap by 0.25 to 0.5 s from beat rounding, so only some same-clip cuts qualify (2 of 12 on Legends).

## Milestone M: the race map and charts (design 2 Oct, morning; defaults in brackets await confirmation)

Today the track viewer is a small offline SVG polyline (400 points, start and finish dots) beside the stats. Make it the place where the race is read as a whole and where the clips are found.

**The map** (full width, stats above it):
- **Zoom and pan: use Leaflet (decided 2 Oct), not our own SVG.** It brings the `+` and `-` buttons, wheel, drag, touch, double click, markers, tooltips, polylines, tile layers and viewport events; a plain Leaflet instance in a small React wrapper (no react-leaflet), with the canvas renderer for the long line. The offline default is Leaflet with no tile layer (the polyline on a plain background); the optional background is a Leaflet tile layer pointed at our tile endpoint. We add "reset" and "zoom to this clip". The line is sent at two levels of detail: about 4,000 points for the whole race and, once zoomed in, the points of the viewport (`/api/track/line?bbox=...&limit=...`), so street-level zoom stays sharp without sending 290,000 points.
- **A marker at the mid-point of every clip** (clip UTC middle placed on the track), labelled with the clip number; **the stretch of track the clip covers is drawn in colour** for its duration (a minimum on-screen length so a 5 s clip is still visible). A clip with no track under it (the pre-start clips) gets no marker and is listed under the map ("not on the track: 0001, 0002, 0003").
- **Hover a marker:** a card with the clip's thumbnail (the "overlay on thumbnails" switch applies) and a few facts: local date and time, length, km and share of the distance, pace, gradient, altitude, heart rate, what the camera sees (settings, weather), number of usable moments, speech seconds. **Click:** open the clip page.
- **Marker colour tells the film's story** [green = played by the newest script draft, grey = not played, dashed = no track]: the map shows at a glance where in the race the film is and where it is thin.
- **Linked cursor:** hovering the track shows the same instant on both charts (and the other way round); dragging a range on a chart highlights that stretch on the map ("show only this section" zooms to it).
- **Background map** [off by default; the offline polyline stays the default]: the Thunderforest and OpenStreetMap tiles that the overlay already caches in `~/.strata360/tiles`, served through `/api/tiles/{style}/{z}/{x}/{y}`, behind the track when the user switches it on (needs the key and the network on first use).

**Two charts under the map** (same horizontal axis and the same markers, hover and zoom):
1. **Elevation**, with a truncated axis (the data's own range plus a margin, not from zero).
2. **Pace** in min/km on a **log scale** with a truncated axis [faster at the top], smoothed (a 20 minute rolling median over the decimated bins) and moving only: stops are gaps, not spikes to infinity; values above a cap (30 min/km [default]) are clipped at the cap.
- **Horizontal axis** [elapsed time in local clock time, with a switch to distance]: time shows the stops and the nights; distance hides them. Day and night are shaded and long stops (over 10 minutes) are marked, since in an 80 hour race they explain the shape.
- Markers on both charts at the same clip times; hover shows the card; click opens the clip. The clip's own duration is a thin band behind the marker.

**Where it lives** [expanded in place on the Overview: stats row, map, then the two charts, full width; the Overview's small map goes]. Alternative: a "Map" entry in the sidebar like the Timeline.

**Data and code:**
- **Backend** (`gps/overview.py` and new `gps/series.py`): `/api/track/series?points=2000` (elapsed seconds, UTC, distance, altitude, pace, heart rate, decimated so peaks survive: min and max per bin for altitude, median for pace); `/api/track/line` (viewport); `/api/track/clips` (per clip: id, number, start and end UTC, mid-point position, the clip's stretch of the line, and `gps/context.py`'s facts; `covered: false` outside the track). Clip positions use the camera clock offset, so the map is also the place to **check the clock**: a marker that sits in the wrong village means the offset is wrong (a later step: nudge the offset while watching the markers).
- **Frontend:** `TrackMap.tsx` (the Leaflet wrapper: markers, hover card, click, the viewport request on `moveend`, the linked cursor marker), `TrackCharts.tsx` (SVG with d3-scale for the shared x scale, the log pace axis and the truncated axes, so the cursor, markers and hover are ordinary DOM and easy to test; `web/src/trackMath.ts` for decimation helpers and axis maths, unit tested), `ClipCard.tsx` (the hover card, shared by map and charts), and `TrackPanel.tsx` composing them with `onOpenClip` from the Workspace.
- **Order:** M1 the three endpoints with tests (decimation, viewport, clip contexts); M2 the map with zoom, pan, markers, hover and click; M3 the charts and the linked cursor; M4 the script colouring, the day / night / stop shading and the optional tile background.
- **Done when:** unit tests: decimation keeps the highest and lowest altitude of each bin; the viewport request returns only points inside it, in order; a clip outside the track is `covered: false`; the log pace axis maps equal ratios to equal distances; Integration tests (msw; Leaflet runs in jsdom with element sizes stubbed): a marker per covered clip, hover shows the card with thumbnail and facts, click calls `onOpenClip`, the zoom buttons change the view and the viewport request follows it, the charts show the same markers, and the cursor on one chart appears on the other.

## Milestone N: generated clips for the gaps (idea 2 Oct; research done, decisions open)

Long stretches of the race have no footage: most nights, and the hours when the runner could not face a camera. They are where narration is most wanted and picture is missing. Idea: **make clips for the gaps**: bird's-eye flights along the GPS track over the terrain, very fast for a whole gap (an hour or more becomes a few seconds to tens of seconds), plus **slower versions of a chosen stretch on demand** where a detailed voice-over needs picture. They enter the auto edit like any other clip.

**What the research found (2 Oct), and what it rules out:**
- *Google Aerial View API:* US postal addresses only, built for buildings and landmarks, not routes ([overview](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/aerial-view/overview)). Not usable for Belgium or for a route.
- *Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles (Map Tiles API):* the policies forbid pre-fetching, storing or caching tiles and any "non-visualization" or offline use, and allow recorded video only as a promotional video of an application you built, at most 30 seconds, marked "for promotional purposes only" ([policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/policies)); customers with an EEA billing address have different terms. Rendering our own flyover film from these tiles is not permitted, so an API key does not solve it.
- *Google Earth Studio* (the web animation tool, KML import works for moderately sized files): no licence for commercial use, "Google Earth" and the data providers must be on screen for the whole duration of the content, and the FAQ mentions no API or scripting ([FAQ](https://www.google.com/earth/studio/faq/)). Usable for a personal, non-commercial film with a watermark, with a manual render step per flyover; not automatable.

**Options (cheapest first):**
1. **N1: an animated 2D map clip made with our own overlay code (decided 2 Oct: first, before any 3D).** No licence question, automatable, deterministic, and the code exists: `overlay/` already draws the clock, distance, pace, altitude, slope and heart rate from the track at any UTC (`series.py`), the whole-route map with a marker, the moving close-up map with automatic zoom (`zoom.py`), over cached tiles (`tiles.py`), with Pillow and OpenCV (5 ms a frame at 1080, 22 ms at 4K). A map clip is those pieces arranged for a whole frame: the close-up map fills the frame and follows the position; the route behind is bright and the route ahead dim; a position marker; the elevation profile along the bottom filling in as the clip runs; the whole-route inset with a progress dot; the clock, distance and pace.
   - **Time and speed:** frame *k* shows UTC `t0 + k * speedup / fps` (the speed-up is the gap's length over the seconds it gets in the film); the zoom follows the *speed on screen*, so the map zooms out when the camera would race across it, and the marker never moves more than a set number of pixels a frame; the position is smoothed.
   - **Pieces:** `gps/gaps.py` (the gap finder: spans between consecutive clips that lie on the track and are longer than a threshold [20 minutes]; for each gap the km range, elapsed time, climb, the share of it spent moving, daylight at each end, the clips either side); `edit/synthetic.py` (the synthetic clips file `synthetic.json`: id `G01`..., kind `map`, start and end UTC, speed-up, seconds, style, key, file, status); `overlay/mapclip.py` (the frame renderer) and a command `strata360 gap-clip FOLDER --gap G01 [--seconds S | --speedup X] [--from T --to T]` that renders to an MP4 with `hw.py`'s encoder (the final film's source for that window is then an ordinary 2D video: see "rendering needs a flat source" below); `strata360 gaps FOLDER` lists the gaps.
   - **Alternative renderer for N1 (idea 3 Oct):** the user's `leaflet-node` library renders a real Leaflet map to PNG on the server (jsdom plus `@napi-rs/canvas`, tile layers, polylines, markers, divIcons and Leaflet plugins work; ([repository](https://github.com/jburnhams/leaflet-node))). A Node script could draw each frame with the same map and styling as the in-app map and pipe the PNGs to ffmpeg, with our tiles served from the cache. It would be slower per frame than the Pillow overlay code and adds Node to the render pipeline, so the overlay code stays the first choice; worth a benchmark before deciding. It could also replace the hand-made jsdom stubs in the Leaflet component tests (`web/tests/utils/media.ts stubLayout`).
   - **Steps:** N1a the gap finder, the synthetic clips file and `gaps` (pure Python, unit tested); N1b the frame renderer and `gap-clip`; N1c the flat-source window in the film render and the time map; N1d the script pack and planner treat synthetic clips as clips (labels such as `G01`; the writer is told "no footage: generated map"); N1e the GUI (a gaps list, generate and slower variants on demand, a stretch chosen on the map or chart of Milestone M).
   - **N1b done:** `overlay/mapclip.py` (`MapClip`, `render`) and `strata360 gap-clip FOLDER --gap G03 [--seconds|--speedup] [--from --to --id] [--size --fps --style]` write `<project>/synthetic/<id>.mp4` and mark the clip `ready` in `synthetic.json`. Frame k shows `t0 + k*speedup/fps`; the camera follows the runner, zoom set so the marker moves ~0.4% of the frame width a frame; the stretch's route red behind and white ahead; elevation strip with cursor; the overlay's clock, distance, pace and whole-route inset on top. Defaults to Thunderforest landscape and fails loudly without a key (`--style osm` picks the key-free map on purpose); a missing key is an error everywhere (thumbnail overlay, final film, gap clips), never a quiet change of map or a map left out. Checked on Legends G03 (1.3 h in 6 s). Gap ids depend on `--min-minutes` (default 20), as in `gaps`.
   - **N1c/N1d done:** a generated clip is a plan window with `synthetic` = its MP4; `render/synthetic.py` plays it in the preview and the final render with no lens and no overlay in the clip itself: the final render puts the film's OWN overlay on it (the same elements and layout as on every camera shot, so the overlay does not change for gaps; user decision 3 Oct) at the race time each frame shows (`synthetic.race_time`: the clip covers `utc_start` to `utc_end` in `synthetic_seconds` of film). No credits are drawn anywhere (user decision 3 Oct): the overlay's `credit` element is off by default (it can still be added to `elements`), and `strata360 credits FOLDER [--write]` lists the credits the film needs (the overlay's map styles, the 2D maps' style, a flyover's imagery and terrain) for the film's distribution. The overlay also has an elevation profile of the whole race along the bottom with the runner's place on it (`profile` element, 120 reference pixels; the bottom row of numbers sits above it), the same on every shot. The script pack lists rendered generated clips as clips labelled `G03` (`SP.norm_label` replaces the `zfill(4)` label rule everywhere), marked NO FOOTAGE, with no words; they are optional for the writer (the 'every clip used or skipped' check ignores them) and are used as `broll` (whole clip) or under `vo`. `script_plan.build` places them between the footage windows (shifting later windows and voice lines), `project.insert_synthetic` writes them into the plan, `framing.resolve` skips them. Still open for N1c: audio of a generated window is silence (music and voice-over only).
   - **Done when (N1a):** unit tests on a synthetic track and clips: gaps are the spans between clips on the track longer than the threshold, overlapping clips merge, spans outside the track are not gaps, the moving share and km range are right, ids are stable for the same clips. **(N1b):** a rendered clip shows the right position at three known moments and its clock runs at the speed-up; no frame moves the marker more than the pixel limit.
2. **N2: a 3D terrain flyover with MapLibre Native (built as a prototype 2 Oct; the camera approved by the user; see `docs/terrain-flyover.md`).** This **supersedes the earlier plan** ("MapLibre GL JS in headless Chromium now, Native when terrain ships"): Native's `feature/terrain-3d` branch (draft [PR 4190](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-native/pull/4190)) builds and renders terrain on Metal here, with no browser and no WebGL-in-headless question. `scripts/flyover/render.py` draws one still per call with `mbgl-render` from a tile cache (`~/.strata360/flyover-cache.db`) and pipes the frames into ffmpeg (about 0.4 s a frame at 720p, run at `nice 19`, one process at a time). **Built:** Esri World Imagery over Mapterhorn terrain; a keyframed camera (zoom and pitch by speed; presets `fast`, `medium`, `slow`, `dive`, or a shots file); the heading fitted on the screen so the route stays in the picture and barely rotates (smoothed, dead-banded), the look-at point panning instead; a terrain-draped arrow marker. **Productionised (2 Oct):** `overlay/flyover.py` (`FlyoverClip`, plan_shots turns a gap into the camera, then the approved camera), `kind: flyover` in `edit/synthetic.py` (4K by default, `size` in the clip and its key), `gap-clip --kind flyover [--size --imagery --no-sharp]`, `kind` and `flyover.available` in the gaps API, a *2D map / 3D flyover (4K)* dropdown per gap in the Gaps panel, unit and web tests; it enters the film as the N1 map clip does. `scripts/flyover/` is removed. **Verified on Linux/OpenGL as well as the Mac (2 Oct):** `mbgl-render` builds and renders on Linux (Mesa software GL under `xvfb-run`), 4K frames and a 4K H.264 clip; sharp tiles are the default (crisper than enlarging 720p tiles, same framing). **Unverified:** Windows, 4K speed on the M4. **Not built:** the route line filling in progressively, and the imagery licence (unread for Esri, Sentinel-2 and the Wallonia orthophotos; needed before any shared film). The engine-independence idea (a style plus a per-frame camera list) is kept as a design goal, not a requirement: the prototype's camera is Python keyframes that the driver turns into `mbgl-render` calls. The decision history follows.
   **Engines considered for N2 (all open source; checked 2 Oct):**
   - **MapLibre GL JS** (BSD; the default pick): the lightest, terrain from raster-dem tiles (Terrain-RGB, easy to make and self-host from an open DEM with `rio-rgbify`), any imagery as XYZ tiles, a camera API, and an existing video-export plugin ([maplibre-gl-video-export](https://github.com/bjperson/maplibre-gl-video-export): deterministic per-frame capture, WebCodecs WebM, up to 4K). Plainest data path.
   - **CesiumJS** (Apache-2.0, free for commercial and non-commercial use; the real rival): a true 3D globe with terrain, 3D Tiles, atmosphere. A GPX flyover already exists as an open-source package ([`@geoblocks/cesium-path-flyover`](https://github.com/geoblocks/cesium-helpers/pull/186): drapes the track on the terrain, chase camera with a sample every 20 m, heading from a 400 m window with damping and rate limiting) and so does a frame-by-frame MP4 export with WebCodecs and no ffmpeg ([PR 197](https://github.com/geoblocks/cesium-helpers/pull/197)). Weaker on self-hosted open data (quantized-mesh terrain is more awkward to build than Terrain-RGB); its easy data comes from the commercial Cesium ion, whose terms are not read yet.
   - **Blender** (GPL; headless `blender -b -P script.py`, Metal on the Mac): a DEM mesh with imagery as its texture, a camera on a path, a sun, haze; the best control over the look and fully offline once the data is prepared, but the most assembly work and the slowest per frame; BlenderGIS can download Google satellite tiles, which we must not use. A candidate for a few hero gaps later.
   - **MapLibre Native** (headless without a browser, Node bindings): attractive, but 3D terrain in Native was still being implemented in its December 2025 newsletter ([newsletter](https://maplibre.org/news/2026-01-03-maplibre-newsletter-december-2025/)): re-check before ruling it out.
   - Not looked at in detail: deck.gl, three.js-based viewers (iTowns), PyVista or QGIS 3D; none has a ready GPX flyover with export, so they are unlikely to beat the first two.
   **Outcome (2 Oct):** MapLibre Native was prototyped first and its look and camera were approved, so CesiumJS, Blender and MapLibre GL JS stay as fall-backs only (GL JS in a browser if Native's terrain branch is not merged and breaks). The imagery licence is the same question for every engine.
3. **N3: Earth Studio, by hand, for a few hero gaps:** we write the KML (camera path along the track, keyframes at the speed we want) and the user renders it in the browser and drops the file in; non-commercial, watermarked, one gap at a time. Worth trying on two or three gaps to see how the look compares with N2.

**How a generated clip enters the edit (the same for all options):**
- A **synthetic clip** in the project: `kind: "synthetic"`, start and end UTC (the gap, or the chosen stretch), the speed-up, the style and parameters (a hash for the cache), the rendered file; no `.OSV`, no sound. The gap finder lists spans between consecutive clips that have track coverage and are longer than a threshold [20 minutes].
- The **script writer** gets them in the context pack as clips with what the track and the places say (km range, pace, altitude, daylight, place names along the way, "no footage: generated flight") so narration can sit on them; the planner gives each a block like any clip. The film's length budget fixes the speed-up (gap seconds divided by the seconds allocated).
- **Rendering needs a flat (non-360) source (built, N1c):** a window from a synthetic clip is an ordinary 2D video scaled to the film (`render/synthetic.py`), played as it is: the map clip has its own overlay baked in at its speed-up, so no piecewise time map is needed in the film render. The same source serves the 3D flyover clips. A flat source is also what imported phone or drone video would need.
- **On demand:** from the map or a chart (Milestone M) select a stretch, choose a speed or a length, and a new variant renders and caches under its key; the draft script can then use it.

**Open decisions:** (N1 is built; N2 is a working prototype, to be connected to the film next); which imagery for N2 and its licence; the gap threshold; whether the film is non-commercial (decides N3); the picture size [same as the film].
- **Done when (N1):** unit tests: the gap finder (spans, threshold, no coverage means no gap); the speed-up arithmetic; the synthetic clip's time map is monotonic and covers the gap once; a synthetic clip is accepted by the script pack and the planner. A rendered 30 s clip of one Legends night shows the right position at three known moments (checked against the overlay's position marker).

## Milestone G: generated music (design 5 Oct; options and plan in `docs/ai-music.md`)

Music built for the film instead of the film fitted to a track: mostly instrumental, so the film's own sound stays clear; any length; intensity that follows the action; the uploaded track's own sung phrases let through only in a few quiet moments (no lyrics of our own). The result is an ordinary music track plus a score (`score.json`), so V5, the rough mix and A3 use it unchanged. The grid is the uploaded track's, so V5 cuts on it without waiting for any of this.
**Steps:** G0 stems (Demucs), re-sequencing the uploaded track to any length and per-bar layering, no generative model (already most of the idea, and the fallback); G1 the intensity curve; G2 the score plan, quiet windows, the original's sung phrases placed by the **director** with a new `sing` draft item (phrase id plus the picture under it; the item is the quiet window), and a **fidelity slider** from "the record" (only the original's audio) to "in its style" (all generated) [default 0.75, "extended"], with per-section pins; G3 ACE-Step 1.5 on the Windows PC for new sections in the original's style; G4 Stable Audio 3 Medium and a bake-off on Legends; G5 fit, check and repair, and the Music panel.
**Decided 5 Oct:** personal film; everything local (a few Gemini Pro calls are fine); no lyrics of our own; tempo and key are the original's; everything runs on the Windows PC (RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB) like the rest of the project; the director chooses the sung moments. **Open:** only the fidelity default (G-D8, [0.75]). **Ready to start (5 Oct):** G0a first (the precise beat tracker shared with V5, key estimation, bar similarity, `edit/remix.py`); see `docs/ai-music.md` 5a.
**Built (5 Oct): G0a.** `music.py` `beat_grid` (dynamic-programming beat tracker that allows drift, refined to the onset peak; every beat and downbeat in seconds), `estimate_key`/`chroma`, `bar_features`/`bar_similarity`; `edit/remix.py` `plan` (bar list to any length: bar 0 first, the track's own last bars last, cheapest joins between similar bars, optional intensity levels) and `render` (equal-power crossfades on the downbeat). Tests: `tests/unit/test_remix.py` (synthetic clicks, drift, chords). **Not yet:** `music.json` does not store the beat list or key; no CLI or endpoint calls it; not yet tried on the Legends track. **Built (5 Oct): G0b.** `edit/stems.py` (Demucs `htdemucs_ft` on ffmpeg-decoded samples, stems kept as FLAC under `music/stems/` keyed by the track's signature; the separator is injectable) and `edit/layers.py` (`layer_gains` per bar in phrases, `open_vocals` windows, `mix` of the re-sequenced stems with ramped gains, `stray_vocal_db` leak check). a bar matching under 0.3 is not judged), `grid_check` (each built bar against its bar of the original; new bars are left to their take's judgement), `tempo_fit` (a take lined up bar by bar with the audio it replaces, or an ideal pulse; fewer than half its bars lining up means make again; the lags' drift is the tempo error, stretched away with ffmpeg `atempo` up to 3% except for a repaint, whose bars around are fixed; a bar still more than 40 ms off means repaint; a repaint is judged on its bars inside the whole context take), `gain_match`, `splice` (the crossfade starts on the downbeat and runs a beat into the section, so the downbeat itself stays on the grid), `fit_length` (cut to the film with a fade when the cut removes audible music, or padded with silence), `singing_db`, `stale`, `repair` (all pending sections in one batch per round, next seed for the ones that failed; a chosen take is not replaced). `audio/music_gen/`: `caption` (the style plus the level's words, always "instrumental, no vocals"), `spec` (the take key covers the section, mode, caption, the context's source bars and levels, backend, model and steps), `params` (repaint in context with the original's instrumental as reference at fidelity 0.75 and above; ## Several tracks per project: runs and routes

A project holds any number of FIT / GPX tracks (`gps/tracks.py`: `tracks.json` manifest, files in `tracks/`; the older single `track.fit|gpx` counts as the first run, id `main`). Each is marked a **run** (the recording, needs times) or a **route** (a planned or official course, may have no times). The pipeline still sees exactly one track through `config.track_path`: the single run's own file, or `track.merged.npz` when there are two or more runs (`merge`: runs in priority order, a lower run only fills the stretches the higher ones did not record without a >30 s break; distance recomputed along the merged line). Routes are informational: only the overview map reads them. Points of interest come from GPX waypoints / named route points and FIT course points (`pois`). GPX is now read with the standard library (`gps/track.py::gpx_parts`; gpxpy was never a listed dependency), including `<rte>` files and heart rate / cadence / temperature extensions.

Server: `GET/POST/DELETE /api/tracks`, `POST /api/tracks/kind`, `GET /api/tracks/line` (any track or `merged`); `/api/track*` is unchanged and serves the race track. Web: a Tracks list in the track panel (multi-file add, run/route select, remove: files are moved to `tracks/removed/`, never deleted) and the overview map draws routes dashed, each run plain when there are several, the merged race track on top, and a marker with a tooltip for each point of interest.

Divergences (`tracks.divergences`): the race track is sampled every 10 m and compared with every route (distance to the nearest route point, routes filled in to 10 m spacing; a KD-tree). Stretches more than 50 m off all routes, less than 150 m apart, are one divergence; the 10 with the largest peak distance are listed in `GET /api/tracks` (`divergences`) and shown on the overview map as a red stretch with an exclamation mark at the farthest point (tooltip: peak distance, length, km of the run).

Divergences are direction-aware: each off-route sample's heading (from the points 30 m either side) is compared with the heading of the route where it is nearest, as lines (the way along it does not matter). A stretch with less than 30% of it heading more than 35° away from the route is only an offset alongside it and is left out; the rest are ranked by peak distance times the share heading the wrong way (`wrong`, `score`).

Route order and checkpoints (`tracks.route_order`): the routes are put in race order from the race track. A route is run in one stretch of the race track about as long as itself, which starts near one of the places the race passes the route's first point (a loop or a start and finish together make several) and goes on or back from there; each start and direction is tried and the stretch lying nearest the route is taken, and the routes are ordered by their stretches, each turned the way the race ran it. The list shows each route's number and the km of the run it covers; a numbered checkpoint is put halfway between the end of each route and the start of the next (they need not meet exactly), none at the start of the first or the end of the last. Checkpoints are in the `pois` of `GET /api/tracks` (`sym: 'checkpoint'`, `n`) and drawn as numbered blue markers.

Highlighting tracks: pointing at a track's row in the Tracks list (or at its line on the map) draws it on the map in bold yellow with a black outline, on top of everything, and shades the row; clicking the name (or the line) keeps it highlighted, several at a time, and clicking again lets go. Lines of tracks not yet drawn are fetched when first highlighted (`GET /api/tracks/line`).

Time at a checkpoint (`tracks.checkpoint_stops`): ONE visit is counted: the pass of the race track through a 300 m zone round the checkpoint that is nearest to where the two routes join along the race (a return to the same place after more than 2 minutes outside the zone is another visit and is not counted). Inside it the speed is taken over 30 s round each sample (a gap while the watch was paused counts as standing still); `arrived` is the first sample slower than 0.7 m/s and `left` the last one of the visit, so time still moving on the way in or out is not counted; `stopped_s` is the time between them. It is in the `stop` of each checkpoint in `GET /api/tracks` and shown in the checkpoint's hover card on the map (clock times in the project timezone).

Overlay clock (`overlay/layout.py::Clock`): top left, the time since the start of the race track in large digits (hours keep counting past 24: 61:01:01), under it the day of the race, and the date and time of day small. Days are calendar days in the project timezone, day 1 being the day the race starts and a new day at each local midnight; a first day shorter than an hour (a start after 23:00) is not a day of its own and goes on into the next calendar day (`race_day`). The distance counter is on the top line to the right of the elapsed time; before the run it reads 0.0 and after it the whole distance.

Run time split (`tracks.timing`): the run, from its first sample to its last, is cut at each checkpoint's arrival and departure (a checkpoint the run never slowed at is a moment). Each route's time is from leaving the previous checkpoint (the run's start for the first route) to arriving at the next (the run's end for the section it ended in); the checkpoint times and the route times add up to the run time exactly, and the Tracks list shows each with the total. Routes beyond the last checkpoint reached have no time; `consistent` is false if the checkpoints came out of order along the run.

Each route in the Tracks list also shows the distance actually run in it, the average pace (the route's time, without the checkpoints', over that distance) and its climb and descent (altitude with a 4 m hysteresis, as for the whole track); the total line gives the whole run's climb and descent.

Map end markers (`tracks.end_markers`): a green start marker at the first point of the run, a chequered finish-line marker at the end of the last route in race order, and a red marker at the last point of the run (tooltip: km and time). Without routes there is no finish line.

Overlay stage text (`overlay/layout.py::Stage`, schedule from `tracks.stage_schedule`): under the date, Before Race (with the time to the start, -4h3m), At Start (until the run is 300 m from where it began), Stage N (km so far / km of the whole stage, time so far / time of the whole stage; a stage runs from leaving the previous checkpoint to arriving at the next), Checkpoint N (km from the start of the run, time there so far / time of the whole stop), At Finish (only if the run came within 300 m of the end of the last route) and After Race (with the time since the end of the run, +34h4m). Distances are from the overlay's own distance counter, so they agree with the big distance figure. Without routes the stage is On Course.

Overlay climb line (`Climb`, `series.running_climb`): a small line under altitude and slope with the total ascent and descent so far (4 m wiggle filter on the smoothed altitude; 0 before the run, the totals after it). It can differ a little from the Tracks list, which uses the raw altitude.

Overlay maps: the whole-route map draws the whole route thin in the medium red, with the part already run over it in a darker red (a patch redrawn when the marker moves on, with the map's see-through-ness); the close-up map draws the route ahead paler and the route behind in the strong colour.

Checkpoint rows in the Tracks list work like the track rows: pointing at one rings that checkpoint on the map in bold yellow, clicking it keeps it highlighted (click again to let go).

Whole-route map lines and marker: lines are drawn at fractions of a pixel (`draw.line_mask`: supersampled), the whole route 1.35 px and the part run 1.65 px (was 1 and 2). The position marker on this map is an arrow (`draw.arrow`) that points the way the route goes on from the marker, looked at 12 map px ahead (not the way the last seconds went), in 5 degree steps; the close-up map keeps its round marker.

Route lines are full opacity: they are layers of their own over the (60% see-through) map pictures, on both maps, with their own `line_opacity` setting in race.json `overlay` (default 1.0). The arrow and the map outline already were.

The close-up map now matches the whole-route map: the whole route in the medium red and the part already run in the darker red, full-opacity lines (it keeps its own thicknesses, 2 and 3 px at 1080p), and the same arrow for the position, pointing along the route a short way ahead (9 map px; `route_bearing`), so it follows bends in the local route rather than the general direction used on the whole-route map (12 map px of a map of the whole race).

Stage text and the route (`tracks.stage_progress`, overlay `Stage`): a stage shows the distance run in it over the length of its route, `X/L km`. Each moment also has the route's own progress: the place on the route nearest the run while the run is within 50 m of it, held at the last such place while it is off the route. When the two differ by more than 10% (and 0.5 km), the route's progress comes first as the normal figure (`P/L km`) and the distance actually run follows in red (`ran X km`), as the bad part. The length and total time of a stage the run did not finish (the last one, no At Finish) are red too, since the run never got to the end of either.

Route distance beside the big distance (`_Ctx.route_elapsed`): next to the small `km` under the big distance counter, `route P / T km`: P is the distance covered along the routes so far, summed over the stages (completed stages in full, plus the progress along the route of the stage in hand, held where the run left the route), and T the total length of all the routes (including stages the run never reached). A run that strayed or missed a loop is behind the distance counter.

With routes, `route P / T km` replaces the small `km` label under the big distance, starting under the start of the big figure (and carrying the unit). Without routes (no route GPX) the overlay is as it was before routes: the small `km` label, and a stage shows the distance run in it over the distance run in the whole stage, all white.

Cut-offs on the overlay (`tracks.overlay_cutoffs`, `_Ctx.cutoff_ahead`): the cut-off of the control the race is heading for is always shown in red as a total time since the start (`CUT-OFF 40:00:00`) beside the day, under the elapsed time: the end of the stage in hand (the first before the start), the checkpoint while at it, the finish at and after it. In the stage line the stage's total time (only known afterwards) is no longer shown: it is the time so far, then, in red, the stage's cut-off counted from leaving the previous checkpoint. Cut-offs are typed per checkpoint and finish (`POST /api/tracks/cutoff`; the input box in the Tracks list is still to be built).

Cut-off box (Tracks list): each checkpoint row and the finish row have a small input; type the cut-off in any common form ("27h", "5:30 from last", "Sat 14:00") and press Enter or leave the box. It shows how it was read (total time, since the previous checkpoint, or time of day), the time since the start it comes to, and the time to spare (green) or late (red) when the run got there; a text that is not understood says why. On the overlay the stage line reads `01:23:45/10:00:00` (time in the stage / its cut-off, in red), the cut-off ahead is on its own line `CUT-OFF 10:00:00` under the elapsed time.

Correction: the `CUT-OFF` line under the elapsed time is the cut-off of the whole race (the finish's), always, not the next control's. In the stage line, with no cut-off set for the stage, the stage's total time is shown (white, `00:14:43/09:22:48`); with one, its cut-off in red replaces it.

Correction to the stage line: when the distance run and the route's progress are within 10% (and 0.5 km) it shows the distance run over the distance run in the whole stage (`46.4/46.9 km`), never actual over the route's length; only when they differ more does it show the route's progress over the route length with `ran X km` in red.

Markers on the overlay maps (`place_badges`, `tracks.overlay_places`): both maps mark the start (green disc with a play triangle), the checkpoints (blue discs with their number) and the finish (a chequered disc, the end of the last route), over the route lines and under the position arrow, which is always on top. When the start and the finish are closer than a badge (a loop) only the finish is drawn. The whole-route map places them once; the close-up map draws those in view (clear of the rounded edge).

Course facts for the script writer (`tracks.race_story`, `story_at`, `story_line`, `story_text`; `script_pack`): the race section of the prompt now has the course (stages, route and distance run in each, time), each checkpoint (when reached, how long stopped, its cut-off and the time to spare or late), the finish (reached or a DNF) and what the run covered of the routes; every clip (and gap) has a `race progress:` line for its middle moment: stage N of M, checkpoints done, route km into the stage, km to the next control, time left to its cut-off (or past it), how far ahead of or behind the last checkpoint's cut-off. The distance run is compared with the route covered only where they differ by more than 10% (and 0.5 km): then the line says it ran X km against Y km of route (detours or off course). Nothing about cut-offs is said until they are typed in.

Gap pages: every gap has a page like a clip, opened from the sidebar (under the clips) or the id in the Gaps panel: the preview of its generated clip with generate / regenerate / remove, how it is drawn (the planner chooses, 2D map or 3D flyover) and how long it is (the plan decides, exactly N seconds, or at least N seconds), must-use in the film, what the newest script draft says over it, and notes with the voice-over MUST INCLUDE (the same fields as a clip: the notes reach the script writer in the pack, and the narration lines become pins inside the gap). The choices are in synthetic.json (`settings`, `synthetic.set_settings`, `POST /api/gaps/settings`) and the planner keeps them: a kind you chose is used (`project.apply_gap_items`, `sync_gap_clips`), a set length is fixed (`script_plan.gap_choice`: the music fit never changes it, narration over it still gets its time), a minimum is never shortened below, and a must-use gap the script left out is added where it falls in the race (`force_gaps`).

Photos (`photos.py`, `/api/photos*`, `PhotosPanel`, `PhotoStrip`): photos taken during the race are added on the overview page (any format Pillow reads, and HEIC / HEIF, converted with pillow-heif or macOS `sips`; the pages always show JPEG copies: `thumb`, up to 4000 px wide). When: the EXIF GPS clock if there is one, else the camera's local time with its UTC offset, else the local time taken to be in the race's time zone (the page says which). Where: the photo's own GPS first, else where the run was at that time; both when it has both, and a warning when they are more than 500 m apart (a wrong camera clock, or taken away from the run), or when the time is outside the run. The time says which clip or gap it was taken in: it is listed on that clip's or gap's page and in the overview list, and has a camera icon at its place on the overview map (hover for the picture, click to open its clip or gap). A photo with no time stamp is refused with the reason. Removed photos are kept under photos/removed.

Photo analysis (`analysis/photo_analysis.py`, `strata360 photos-analyse`, `POST /api/photos/analyse`): the clip stages that make sense for a still photo are run over the uploaded photos, each redone only when its version, the photo or what it reads changes (or with force): `exposure` (luma, the share clipped, the spread in stops), `quality` (detail, blur, haze, contrast, saturation; featureless / blurry / hazy), `places` (the address and named places within a kilometre at the photo's position, with the race facts at that moment), `people` (YOLO pose persons and InsightFace faces with head pose and embeddings; `.venv-vision`, `analysis/photo_detect.py`), `identity` (which face is the wearer by the face profile, and how many people are in shot), `face_view` (is the wearer's face clear), `scenes` (the local vision model: setting, light, weather, tags, scenery 1-10 and clarity; one model load for all the photos) and `thumb_overlay` (the picture with the race overlay as at the time it was taken; none for a photo outside the run). The clip stages with nothing to work on in a still are ingest, the sound stages (extract, clean, background, events, levels, transcribe, align, transcript_check, speakers), motion, proxy, thumb / thumb_best and candidates. A stage that cannot run (no wearer profile, no models) fails loudly after the others have been tried. Results are in photos/analysis/<id>.json; the overview and the clip and gap pages show them on each photo, and the panel has Analyse photos / Redo all with the progress.

Pan and zoom on photos (`edit/photo_motion.py`, `/api/photos/motion*`, `PhotoMotion`): a move is a camera path over the photo, a 16:9 window whose centre and zoom change with a smooth ease, always inside the photo and never zoomed past `max_upscale` (1.5x) of its real pixels. It is aimed at what the photo analysis found, most important first: your face, other faces and persons, the objects (not tiny), the most detailed block of the picture (a saliency measure), else the thirds. Styles: push in (whole picture to the subject), pull out, pan across (the long way of a wide or tall photo, ending on the side the subject is on; a photo with no room is zoomed in a little first), drift (one point of interest to the next), reveal (a detail at high zoom, widening to the next) and hold (a slight breath); `auto` picks by the shape of the photo and what is in it, the seed choosing among the good ones and the direction when nothing decides (the same seed always gives the same move). Each photo card has Pan & zoom: the style, the length (2 to 30 s), another take, the start (green) and end (red) windows drawn on the picture, Watch (an MP4 made when first asked for and kept in photos/motion/) and Use this move, which keeps the choice in photos.json. Using the moves in the plan and the render of the film is the next step.

Objects (`analysis/photo_objects.py`, the photo stage `objects`): the everyday objects in a photo (YOLO11 on its 80 classes, people left to `people`) with boxes, shown on the card and used as pan and zoom targets; not run for clips.

Photos in the film (`script_pack.photo_clips`, `edit/photo_clip.py`, the `photo` script item): every photo is a pack clip `P1`... in time order with the clips and gaps, described in the prompt (when and where, the scene, place, scenery, who is in it, the objects, how it looks) and marked THE RUNNER WANTS THIS PHOTO IN THE FILM when its card says Use in the film (`photos.json` `must`). The writer may use one with a `photo` item (2.5 to 12 s; the editor pans and zooms on it), put a `vo` item over it, or leave it out, and a must-use photo with no item is a problem that sends the draft back. The plan treats a photo as a picture-only generated clip: `photo_clip.sync` renders its move (the photo's style and seed, aimed by the analysis) to a 4K video of exactly the whole-beat window the plan gives it (kept while the photo, the length and the move are unchanged), `synthetic.json` holds it as kind `photo` (t0 = t1 = when it was taken, so the overlay shows the race as it was then), and the preview and final film play it like a gap clip. A must-use photo the script leaves out is added where its time falls (`force_gaps`, with the gaps marked must), at the length of its move; removing a photo removes its clip.

Photo length and move (correction): a photo is shown for 2 to 3 s, not as long as a gap: `photo_analysis.auto_seconds` gives 2 s to a plain picture and 3 s to a busy one (`busyness`: the fine detail and how many people, faces and objects are in it), the plan fits it to the music between 2 and 3 s, and a length you set on the photo (2 to 8 s) is kept exactly. The pan and zoom depends on the length: a shot under 3.5 s only gets a push in, a pull out or a hold (a very wide or tall photo is still panned), and every move is made in proportion to its time (all of it at 6 s or more, a third of it at 2 s) so it is not too fast to watch.

**OpenStreetMap lookup service and road stretches.** `gps/osm.py` is a cached lookup service for the Overpass API: a pool of eight public mirrors used in round robin (a mirror at most once per 1.1 s, a failing mirror rests 20 s doubling to 10 min and is forgiven by its next success), parallel `query_many`, and every good answer kept on disk (a failure is never cached; cache files are named like the places stage's, so its existing answers still hit). The places stage now asks the pool when it uses the public server (a server set in race.json is still asked alone). `gps/roads.py` `stretches(lat, lon, dist, cache_dir)` returns the runs of a track (sampled every 20 m) that are within 8 m of a drivable road for at least 300 m, fetched per 0.1 degree tile. Tried on the Legends track: 36 stretches, 23.9 km, in about 160 s over a cold cache. This is the base for a street-view gap option (Mapillary token and Google Maps key are in `secrets.env`, not yet used by the app).

**Street view page.** `streetview.py` keeps in `<race_dir>/streetview/` one stage for the road parts of the run (`roads.json`, from `gps/roads.py` and the cached Overpass mirror pool) and one stage for each of three providers (`mapillary.json`, `panoramax.json`, `google.json`). A provider stage records the SECTIONS of imagery on the road parts: one per capture run (a Mapillary sequence, a Panoramax collection, a run of Google panoramas) along one stretch, split where the frames leave a gap over 60 m. A section is `360` (a panoramic camera, sees every way) or `2d` (a flat camera: each frame has its angle to the runner's way, counted as forward / right / back / left), with km range, length, picture count, spacing, years, camera and native size. Frames count only within 12 m of the run's line. A provider stage is redone when the roads change (it records the roads' id). `strata360 streetview F [--stages ...] [--force]` runs them; the server runs the same in the background (`/api/streetview`, `/run`, `/image`); the keys (`MAPILLARY_TOKEN`, `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` in secrets.env) never reach the browser. Mapillary and Panoramax pictures are kept in `streetview/img/` (CC BY-SA, credit them); Google's are fetched for display each time and never kept (its terms). The web page (under Overview in the sidebar) shows the stages with Run / Redo, a map with the road parts (click one) and a marker per section (provider colour, "360" or "2D"), the pictures of the chosen section (one per way a flat camera faced), filters, and the sections as a list. Next: offer a section to the director and as a gap style.

**Street view: choosing sections.** `streetview.annotate` gives every section a stable key (`provider:sequence:km`), whether it is plausible (at least 30 pictures, 150 m, pictures no more than 10 m apart; Google's are never offered, its terms), its play time at 15 pictures a second and the apparent speed, the ids of the sections over the same road (overlap of at least 30% of the shorter one: another source or date) and your choice. The page lists the plausible sections with details, three previews, the overlap warning and a choice: not used / possible / must include, saved in `streetview/choices.json` (`POST /api/streetview/choice`). Only chosen sections go on to the script writer and the plan (next).

**Street view in the film.** A section you mark possible or must include (page: Street view) gets a permanent label V1, V2 ... (`streetview/choices.json`). Only those go on: `script_pack.streetview_clips` makes each a pack clip (no footage, no words; where it is on the course, the camera, when it was filmed and whether that light fits the race passing there, the sections over the same road) and the prompt offers a `streetview` item whose length is between 2 s and the section's slowest smooth speed (4 pictures a second blended to 30 frames a second; there is no fastest: pictures are skipped, so any clip down to 2 s plays the whole stretch). `script_draft.check` holds the item to that range, requires the must-include ones and refuses two over the same road; `script_plan` places it like a photo (a generated picture-only piece, fitted to the music within its range, added in its place when marked must and left out); `project.plan_from_script` makes the clips with `edit/streetview_clip.sync` (kind `streetview`, covering the race minutes of the section, so the overlay runs through them). Credits name Mapillary / Panoramax (CC BY-SA); a photo clip no longer gets a map credit. The camera (`edit/streetview_cam.py`): Mapillary 360 uses the true rotation Mapillary reconstructed for each picture to level the horizon, heading along the smoothed path of the camera positions, looking 25 m ahead; Panoramax 360 estimates which way is up from the picture (about 3 degrees) and heads along the road; flat cameras use the pictures that face forward, steadied by matching the far field of neighbours and cropping in, and may still wobble (the page says so). The pictures between two real ones are made by moving both along the optical flow. Settings were chosen by measuring the far-field shift between the finished clip's frames (`jit.py` in the experiment folder): heading from the camera path (not the road line), smoothing 4 pictures, look-ahead 25 m for Mapillary; 1.5 pictures of smoothing for the estimated up and 5 for the heading for Panoramax. Not done: the daytime check is only a warning in the prompt, frame-to-frame matching did not help the 360 clips (too few matches between pictures 7 to 15 m apart), and different virtual cameras (glances toward objects exist in the prototype, not here).

**Street view quality score.** `edit/streetview_cam.py` `quality` measures how good a clip of a section will look without making it, and `streetview.run` stage `quality` does it for every plausible Mapillary / Panoramax section (on the smaller 2048 wide copies; a section already scored is kept, one whose pictures changed is scored again). Two measurements: the in-between PSNR (for sample pictures the middle one is rebuilt from its two neighbours by the flow blend and compared with the real one: this falls with picture spacing, bad levelling and things moving near the road) and the steadiness of a 4 s test clip at 25 m/s (the far field followed frame to frame: jerk and turning). The score is 0 to 100 (half in-between, a third jerk, the rest turning; thresholds `SCORE_*`) and graded good (65+), fair (40+) or poor. Measured on the three samples it ranked them the way a person did: Mapillary 360 78 (good), Panoramax 360 63 (fair), the flat dashcam section 9 (poor). The page shows the grade on each candidate with the measurements on hover; it does not block a poor one. A first version measured the shake between source pictures, which the forward motion itself inflates (44 degrees for the Panoramax section): measure on the frames of a clip played at a fixed road speed instead, so sections of different spacing compare fairly.

**Street view page, later additions.** A Filters menu (quality, steadiness, light, position among the footage, usable); for each section when it was filmed and when the runner passed it (race time zone), the nearest camera clip each way along the run with the distance and the time, the clips it overlaps and the gap it fills; the light warning from the sun's height at the real dates and place of the filming and of the pass (both heights in the message); a preview video made in the background, kept (named by the section and the camera version) and played on the page. **Clicking the map** asks Mapillary, Panoramax and Google for the nearest street view to that point as the crow flies, whatever the run did there (`streetview.near_point`: the nearest capture runs, searched 100, 250 then 500 m round; Google's nearest panoramas by asking at and round the point), each with the rules that would rule it out written out as they apply to a section: how far it is from the run's track, whether the run was on a road there, which way a flat camera faced, the pictures nearby against what a clip needs, the light, whether it overlaps camera footage. No filters apply (nothing about Google's terms: whether to use Google is your choice); the pictures are fetched only for what that search found.
