# Insta360 X3 support (13 Oct 2026)

Added the Insta360 `.insv` pair as a second camera behind the same interface as the DJI `.OSV`: discovery, ingest, telemetry, lens calibration and decoding. Code: `src/strata360/osv/insta360.py` (the reader), `osv/source.py` (how a lens is opened), dispatch in `osv/calib.py`, `telemetry.py`, `meta.py`, `pipeline/clips.py`, `pipeline/ingest.py`. Sample: 61 clips of an X3 (`D:\Insta360\2024-07-20 - Crossing Switzerland`).

## Learned (from the sample; all measured, not assumed)

- **Files.** A recording is two MP4s, one per lens: `VID_<date>_<time>_00_NNN.insv` is the REAR lens (the wearer is in it), `_10_` the FRONT lens, and `LRV_..._11_NNN.lrv` a 1024x512 dual-fisheye proxy. Each is H.264, 2880x2880, 29.97 fps, full-range `yuvj420p`, AAC audio (in `_00_`). The two lens files can differ by a frame (479 against 478): the shorter decides. Only `_00_` carries the metadata trailer.
- **Trailer.** After the MP4 data: records of (data, u16 id, u32 length) read backwards from 78 bytes before the end; the last 40 bytes are u32 length, u32 version (3) and the 32-byte magic `8db42d69...`. Ids: `0x101` protobuf (serial, model, firmware, lens calibration string in field 54, 3x3 IMU matrix in field 60), `0x300` IMU, `0x400` exposure time per frame. `0x200`/`0x500` are H.264 previews. No GPS record in these clips.
- **IMU.** 1 kHz, 20 bytes a sample: u64 microseconds, then six offset-binary u16: accelerometer x y z, gyroscope x y z. Gyro scale 2000 deg/s over 32768 (fits to within 1% on three clips). Same axes for both sensors; gravity is along -x when the camera is upright.
- **Camera to body.** Matching image rotation (optical flow on lens frames, unprojected through the lens model, Kabsch) to integrated gyro, on three clips: correlation 0.97, and the IMU-to-lens rotation is a clean signed permutation (residual under 1 degree). Body frame as DJI's (+Y front, +Z up the stick): x_b = z_imu, y_b = y_imu, z_b = -x_imu.
- **Clock.** The IMU and exposure share the camera clock. The first video frame is at (second exposure record + 0.2336 s) (0.223 to 0.234 s on three clips). Exposure records start 20 frames early and end a few late.
- **Lens model.** The calibration string `2_<lens>_<lens>_<n>` is a unified-camera (Mei) model per lens (xi 1.948, fx 4637, k1..k3, p1, p2, second lens 2.67 cm away) in a 5952-px lens image; the 2880-px video is that halved and cropped 48 px each side. Verified by stitching: with that scale the lens-to-lens rotation fits at 179.8 degrees about the vertical axis with 0.2 to 0.5 degrees rms (any other scale gives 160 to 175). The usable circle runs to about 105 degrees from the axis.
- **Time.** The container `creation_time` is 9 to 10 s EARLIER than the file name; the file name agrees with the file's modification time less the clip length, so the file name is the start (ingest uses it for Insta360; the camera clock is local time, so `--clock-offset-hours 2` for Switzerland in July).

## How it plugs in

The renderers and analysers all work in the DJI conventions (two Kannala-Brandt lenses in 3840-px lens coordinates, body-to-world quaternions, `d_body = B^T R(q)^T P^T d_world`). Rather than fork them, the adapter speaks that language: the Mei lenses are fitted to Kannala-Brandt terms (worst error 0.3 px of 3840), the gyro and accelerometer are fused (Mahony filter) into per-frame upright quaternions so `imu_offsets(osv)` is the identity for Insta360, and decoding hands each lens over at its own size (the GPU path converts full to limited range in `source.full_frame_filter`; nothing is resized). Every ffmpeg decode of a lens goes through `source.lens_input`.

First stitched frame through the unmodified renderer: seams invisible, horizon level, wearer in the rear lens.

## Limits and to do

- Yaw drifts with the gyro's bias (no magnetometer); the clip's median bias is removed. Heading comes from the GPS track as before.
- Colour temperature is not recorded (0 in `read_exposure`); ISO is read from record `0x900`.
- Lens alignment: the nominal 180 degree flip was about 1.2 degrees out (see Seam evaluation); the X3's measured correction is applied in `insta360.slots`. It is per camera, so other X3 units may differ a little and other models get none.
- Only the X3 was seen. Other models (X4: 8K, different trailer fields) need their own check; `parse_calibration` raises on an unknown string.
- Proxy rendering on a machine without a GPU is slow (the seam carving and parallax run per frame).

## Seam evaluation (14 Oct 2026)

Corpus: 3 frames from each of the 61 clips scored for seam disagreement (`spike/insta360/`), then the 16 worst and 16 best kept as a frozen set. Findings:
- Frame pairing by index is right: pairing the rear frame with the front frame one earlier or later made both groups worse. No lens timing offset to fix.
- The first metric (brightness difference between the lenses) mostly measured glare and exposure: a sun glow seen by one lens only scored as a bad seam. Replaced by 1 - correlation of high-passed pictures, which ignores those and responds to edges not lining up.
- The nominal lens-to-lens rotation was about 1.2 degrees out. Fitted from SIFT matches in the overlap (rms 1.59 to 1.16 degrees) and refined against the metric with a held-out half (focal scale of the front lens +0.4 percent): the metric fell from 0.80 to 0.62 on the good frames and 0.77 to 0.54 on the bad, 30 of 32 frames improved with the first fit, and its inverse made all worse. The remaining mismatch is mostly parallax from the 2.7 cm lens spacing.
- Obstructed lens (clip 007: a hand or cloth over the lens) and flare (042) are footage problems; a better stitch cannot fix them.
- Stitched views at both seams show no visible discontinuity on three sets (two of them the worst scorers). The stick is not visible straight down; it shows as a thin line only where it crosses in front of the wearer.

### Carving, parallax and the glare step (14 Oct 2026)
Rendered the seams of the worst corpus sets with plain feathering, carving plus parallax, and carving plus the exposure-match gain. Geometry holds in all three: no doubled edges at either seam. The visible seam in the bad frames (009, 034, 062) is a brightness step: the sun puts a veiling haze into one lens only, with a straight edge where the haze meets the lens limit. Carving makes that step sharper (it picks a clean join, not a matching brightness); the gain trims it a little (034) and does nothing for 009 and 062. A two-band blend (low frequencies mixed over about 40 px at 800 px wide, fine detail over the narrow seam, in linear light) turns the hard edge into a soft gradient and keeps detail sharp (`spike/insta360/mband.py`). Not in the renderer yet: it is shared with the DJI path, so it should be an option first.

### Two-band blend and colour match: not adopted (14 Oct 2026)
Implemented an opt-in two-band seam blend in the CPU renderer (low frequencies mixed over about 12 degrees, detail over the narrow seam, wide weights kept inside the region both lenses see), and a variant that first scales one lens's low band by the smooth ratio of the two around the seam (to even out exposure and colour). Compared with plain feathering and the existing global exposure-match gain on the 32-frame Insta360 corpus and one frame of each of the 25 DJI clips of the Legends folder (both seams, 800 px views). Metric: the 95th-percentile gradient of the output luminance (smoothed to 1.5 degrees) in the seam band, relative to the lenses' own gradient there; detail kept = fine-scale energy in the band against feather.
- Two-band: median step unchanged; on the Insta360 bad sets worse than feather on 10 of 16 and better on none, on DJI worse on 2 and better on none. The hard diagonal edge in clips 009 and 062 is a flare shaft inside one lens's own picture and only partly lies in the transition, so blending cannot remove it.
- Colour match: no better than two-band (better on 3 of 16 bad sets, worse on 9).
- Global gain (already in the renderer, off by default): the only variant that helped, 10 of 16 Insta360 bad sets better and 2 worse, DJI 3 better and none worse, good sets unchanged. Small samples, and the earlier DJI finding that its statistics are unreliable on wet-lens clips stands, so the default is left alone.
- The extreme steps are covered-lens frames (Insta360 007, DJI 0025) and a black night frame (DJI 0006): footage, not stitching.
The patch is kept in `spike/insta360/multiband.patch`; the renderer is unchanged.

## Native-size lenses (14 Oct 2026)
The pipeline no longer assumes a 3840 px lens. A source's lens size is the width of its video (`osv/source.py` `lens_size`, by ffprobe, cached): 3840 on the DJI, 2880 on the X3. Decoders, frame readers, occlusion maps (the feather scales with the size), the exposure and view analysis scaling, the object detector's "tiny" threshold and the upscale planner's pixels-per-degree all use it; Insta360 lens calibration is now in the video's own pixels (`insta360.canvas_to_video`), and nothing is upscaled to fit. Checked: a DJI frame renders bit-identically before and after; an Insta360 frame matches the old upscaled route at 44 dB PSNR with no shift; the exposure and proxy stages and the integration suite pass. Not done: the GPU renderer (`render/gpu.py`) was not run (no CUDA here); it takes frame sizes from the arrays and the `side` argument, so it should follow, but check on an NVIDIA machine.

## ISO found, colour temperature not (14 Oct 2026)
Record `0x900` is the auto-exposure table (Gyroflow's telemetry-parser calls record 9 `AAAData`; our trailer ids are record number in the high byte, format in the low): 48-byte entries about every 4 frames, u32 millisecond timestamp, f32 target EV, f32 exposure ms (matches `0x400`), u32 stats, u32 packed luma stats with ISO = `100 * (bits >> 19) >> 6`, then unused words. Checked on real clips: ISO about 3100 on a dusk clip with the shutter at its 30 ms limit, 110 to 350 by day, 110 in bright light. `read_exposure` now reports it per frame (it was a constant 100), so the brightness proxy `log2(shutter / ISO)` the sun/clock check uses works for Insta360 too. There is no white balance or colour temperature field in the format as documented, and none that I could find in the unused words, so colour temperature stays 0. Record `0xa00` is the anchors table (empty here).

## The whole X3 library (20 Oct 2026)
Ran discovery, ingest, calibration, telemetry and exposure over 3 clips from each of the 42 Insta360 folders on the owner's drive (119 clips), then the IMU and MP4 index of all 885 recordings. Script in the session scratchpad; the checks that matter are now `tests/integration/test_real_footage.py` (opt-in, `STRATA_REAL_FOOTAGE`).
- **One camera.** Every recording is the same X3 (one serial), on 8 firmware versions from v1.0.04 (2022) to v1.0.155 (2025). The trailer, calibration (identical fit on all), IMU scale and axes, 1 kHz rate, 29.97 fps, 2880 px lenses and the "file name is the start, creation_time about 10 s early" rule hold on all of them. The measured lens correction therefore fits all this footage; no other unit or model has been seen.
- **Cut-short recordings.** 4 X3 pairs (Art Birth, 2 Oct 2022) and 1 Osmo 360 clip (Beacons 100, 0056) have no `moov` box: the battery or card ran out. ffprobe failed on them in ingest with a bare CalledProcessError. Discovery now reports them as unsupported with that reason (`clips.NO_INDEX`, `mp4.cut_short`: an MP4 that starts with `ftyp` and has no `moov`), so they never reach a stage.
- **IMU written twice.** 5 clips have one place where the IMU clock steps back 4 to 14 ms (the same samples written again); `frames_from_imu` then failed ("Times must be in strictly increasing order"). `read_imu` keeps only samples later than every one before them.
- **IMU holes.** 56 clips have a stretch of 0.2 to 1 s with no IMU samples, about one second into the video. The filter carried the last rate across it (np.gradient spreads the hole over the samples on each side), so a camera swinging at the time came out tilted by up to 66 degrees afterwards (mismatch with the accelerometer's 'up' in the 0.5 s after the hole, 20 clips: median 15 degrees). It now holds the orientation across a hole and re-levels from the next 0.2 s of accelerometer (median 9 degrees, worst 28; 15 of 20 better, the rest within 4 degrees). The Insta360 telemetry cache is renamed (`frames-insta360-2`) so existing projects pick this up.
- Most folders also hold flat `.mp4` exports, `.gyro`, `.json` and `.wav` files beside the camera originals; they are reported and ignored as before (GPS tracks, Insta360 Studio projects and audio now get their own labels).
- Clip length: see overview 12, question 3.

## Recovering cut-short recordings (20 Oct 2026)
`strata360 recover <folder>` (`osv/recover.py`) rebuilds the index of every cut-short camera file in a footage folder as a copy in `<folder>/strata360/recovered/`, under the original name; the originals are only read. Discovery then uses the copy (`Clip.recovered`, a note in clip.json) and its proxy, recovered too when it was also cut short.
- **How.** Both cameras write the media data in a fixed pattern, one sample a chunk, back to back. X3: one H.264 frame (a single NAL unit), then the AAC frames due by then (exactly floor(k * 1.5641 + c) before video frame k, the same c on every clip checked). DJI: two `djmd` and two `dbgi` samples (each one protobuf field), then the front and rear HEVC frames, with runs of AAC frames. A good clip of the same kind from the same folder, recorded nearest in time, is the template: its moov gives the codec setup, timing and the order of the tracks; the cut file's media is walked in that order, video frame ends are found from the NAL units, metadata ends from the protobuf, and an audio run is split at frame starts that decode as exactly one AAC frame of 1024 samples (PyAV). The copy is the original bytes, the mdat size filled in, then the template's moov with the new sample tables, the recording's own start time (from its file name) and without the template's user data.
- **Checked.** Walking good clips reproduces their own index sample for sample (4 X3 lens files and proxies, 2 DJI clips, all 7 DJI tracks); the recovered copies decode without an error at both ends, the proxies in full; the DJI copy at 60 s matches the intact proxy at 60 s.
- **What came back.** Art Birth (2 Oct 2022): 4 X3 pairs and their proxies, 7 s, 5 min 38 s, 10 s and 2 min 34 s. Beacons 100, clip 0056: 75 s of the 105 s; after that the file holds only zeros (the camera reserved the space and never wrote the frames), so the last 30 s exist only in the LRF proxy, which is intact.
- **Limits.** An X3 writes its IMU, exposure and calibration in the trailer after the index, so a recovered X3 clip has none: the trailer is rebuilt with the template's camera record (same camera: model, serial, firmware, lens calibration) only, and the clip is treated as held upright (identity orientation, no horizon levelling) with ISO and shutter unknown (100 and 1/100), as clip.json says. A DJI clip keeps its telemetry, which is part of the media data. Only whole frames are kept: the last partial frame and any audio after the last video frame are left out.
