# Insta360 X3 support (13 Oct 2026)

Added the Insta360 `.insv` pair as a second camera behind the same interface as the DJI `.OSV`: discovery, ingest, telemetry, lens calibration and decoding. Code: `src/strata360/osv/insta360.py` (the reader), `osv/source.py` (how a lens is opened), dispatch in `osv/calib.py`, `telemetry.py`, `meta.py`, `pipeline/clips.py`, `pipeline/ingest.py`. Sample: 61 clips of an X3 (`D:\Insta360\2024-07-20 - Crossing Switzerland`).

## Learned (from the sample; all measured, not assumed)

- **Files.** A recording is two MP4s, one per lens: `VID_<date>_<time>_00_NNN.insv` is the REAR lens (the wearer is in it), `_10_` the FRONT lens, and `LRV_..._11_NNN.lrv` a 1024x512 dual-fisheye proxy. Each is H.264, 2880x2880, 29.97 fps, full-range `yuvj420p`, AAC audio (in `_00_`). The two lens files can differ by a frame (479 against 478): the shorter decides. Only `_00_` carries the metadata trailer.
- **Trailer.** After the MP4 data: records of (data, u16 id, u32 length) read backwards from 78 bytes before the end; the last 40 bytes are u32 length, u32 version (3) and the 32-byte magic `8db42d69...`. Ids: `0x101` protobuf (serial, model, firmware, lens calibration string in field 54, 3x3 IMU matrix in field 60), `0x300` IMU, `0x400` exposure time per frame. `0x200`/`0x500` are H.264 previews. No GPS record in these clips.
- **IMU.** 1 kHz, 20 bytes a sample: u64 microseconds, then six offset-binary u16: accelerometer x y z, gyroscope x y z. Gyro scale 2000 deg/s over 32768 (fits to within 1% on three clips). Same axes for both sensors; gravity is along -x when the camera is upright.
- **Camera to body.** Matching image rotation (optical flow on lens frames, unprojected through the lens model, Kabsch) to integrated gyro, on three clips: correlation 0.97, and the IMU-to-lens rotation is a clean signed permutation (residual under 1 degree). Body frame as DJI's (+Y front, +Z up the stick): x_b = z_imu, y_b = y_imu, z_b = -x_imu.
- **Clock.** The IMU and exposure share the camera clock. The first video frame is at (second exposure record + 0.2336 s) (0.223 to 0.234 s on three clips). Exposure records start 20 frames early and end a few late.
- **Lens model.** The calibration string `2_<lens>_<lens>_<n>` is a unified-camera (Mei) model per lens (xi 1.948, fx 4637, k1..k3, p1, p2, second lens 2.67 cm away) in a 5952-px lens image; the 2880-px video is that halved and cropped 48 px each side. Verified by stitching: with that scale the lens-to-lens rotation fits at 179.8 degrees about the vertical axis with 0.2 to 0.5 degrees rms (any other scale gives 160 to 175). The usable circle runs to about 105 degrees from the axis.
- **Time.** The container `creation_time` is 9 to 10 s EARLIER than the file name; the file name agrees with the file's modification time less the clip length, so the file name is the start (ingest uses it for Insta360; the camera clock is local time, so `--clock-offset-hours 2` for Switzerland in July).

## How it plugs in

The renderers and analysers all work in the DJI conventions (two Kannala-Brandt lenses in 3840-px lens coordinates, body-to-world quaternions, `d_body = B^T R(q)^T P^T d_world`). Rather than fork them, the adapter speaks that language: the Mei lenses are fitted to Kannala-Brandt terms (worst error 0.3 px of 3840), the gyro and accelerometer are fused (Mahony filter) into per-frame upright quaternions so `imu_offsets(osv)` is the identity for Insta360, and decoding scales each lens to 3840x3840 (and for the GPU path converts full to limited range) in `source.full_frame_filter`. Every ffmpeg decode of a lens goes through `source.lens_input`.

First stitched frame through the unmodified renderer: seams invisible, horizon level, wearer in the rear lens.

## Limits and to do

- Yaw drifts with the gyro's bias (no magnetometer); the clip's median bias is removed. Heading comes from the GPS track as before.
- ISO and colour temperature are not recorded: ISO is a constant 100 and colour temperature 0 in `read_exposure`, so the exposure-based sun/clock check is weaker. `0x900` (not decoded) may hold more.
- Decoding upscales 2880 to 3840 on the CPU: correct but wasteful; a native-size renderer (`LS` per source) would save it.
- Lens alignment: the nominal 180 degree flip was about 1.2 degrees out (see Seam evaluation); the X3's measured correction is applied in `insta360.slots`. It is per camera, so other X3 units may differ a little and other models get none.
- Only the X3 was seen. Other models (X4: 8K, different trailer fields) need their own check; `parse_calibration` raises on an unknown string.
- Proxy rendering on a machine without a GPU is slow (the seam carving and parallax run per frame).

## Seam evaluation (14 Oct 2026)

Corpus: 3 frames from each of the 61 clips scored for seam disagreement (`spike/insta360/`), then the 16 worst and 16 best kept as a frozen set. Findings:
- Frame pairing by index is right: pairing the rear frame with the front frame one earlier or later made both groups worse. No lens timing offset to fix.
- The first metric (brightness difference between the lenses) mostly measured glare and exposure: a sun glow seen by one lens only scored as a bad seam. Replaced by 1 - correlation of high-passed pictures, which ignores those and responds to edges not lining up.
- The nominal lens-to-lens rotation was about 1.2 degrees out. Fitted from SIFT matches in the overlap (rms 1.59 to 1.16 degrees) and refined against the metric with a held-out half (focal scale of the front lens +0.4 percent): the metric fell from 0.80 to 0.62 on the good frames and 0.77 to 0.54 on the bad, 30 of 32 frames improved with the first fit, and its inverse made all worse. The remaining mismatch is mostly parallax from the 2.7 cm lens spacing.
- Obstructed lens (clip 007: a hand or cloth over the lens) and flare (042) are footage problems; a better stitch cannot fix them.
- Stitched views at both seams show no visible discontinuity on three sets (two of them the worst scorers). The stick is not visible straight down; it shows as a thin line only where it crosses in front of the wearer.

### Carving, parallax and the glare step (14 Oct 2026)
Rendered the seams of the worst corpus sets with plain feathering, carving plus parallax, and carving plus the exposure-match gain. Geometry holds in all three: no doubled edges at either seam. The visible seam in the bad frames (009, 034, 062) is a brightness step: the sun puts a veiling haze into one lens only, with a straight edge where the haze meets the lens limit. Carving makes that step sharper (it picks a clean join, not a matching brightness); the gain trims it a little (034) and does nothing for 009 and 062. A two-band blend (low frequencies mixed over about 40 px at 800 px wide, fine detail over the narrow seam, in linear light) turns the hard edge into a soft gradient and keeps detail sharp (`spike/insta360/mband.py`). Not in the renderer yet: it is shared with the DJI path, so it should be an option first.
