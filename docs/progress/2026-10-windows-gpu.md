# Windows port and the GPU renderer (10 Oct 2026)

The project was developed on an M4 Mac; this machine is a Ryzen 9 5950X (16 cores), 64 GB, RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB), Windows 11, ffmpeg 8.1 (gyan) with NVENC and D3D11VA.

## Setup

- Python 3.13 venv (`scripts/setup_env.ps1`; the 3.14 on the machine has no pinned wheels), torch 2.9.1+cu128 (Blackwell needs CUDA 12.8 or newer), nvidia-cublas-cu12 / cudnn for ctranslate2 (`hw._cuda_dll_dirs` puts their DLLs on the search path). `strata360.cmd` replaces the bash launcher.
- faster-whisper on CUDA float16 (`hw.whisper_args`), decode with D3D11VA (3x faster than software on the 3840x3840 10-bit HEVC lenses), encode with `hevc_nvenc`. NVENC refuses frames under about 144 px, so the integration tests force `STRATA_ENCODER=software`.
- A project made on the Mac stores the OSV paths of the Mac (`/Volumes/Expansion/...`): `config.source` finds the file of that name beside the project. The map overlay needs `THUNDERFOREST_API_KEY` in `secrets.env` (not in the repo); `STRATA_NO_OVERLAY=1` renders without it.

## Findings (measured, 1080p, 50 fps, the same clip)

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Start: CPU renderer on Windows | 0.38 fps. Not the maths: raw 3840x3840 rgb48 frames through an anonymous pipe (4 KB buffer on Windows) went at 80 MB/s, 1.2 s a frame. |
| Named pipe (`oslib.big_pipe`, used by `guard.popen` for every `rawvideo` stream) | 0.03 s a frame; renderer 2.4 fps (6x). A few MB of buffer is as fast as 256 MB (and 256 MB x a dozen pipes exhausted the pool). |
| Seam: numba for `solve_dp` | 72 ms to 1.75 ms, identical path. |
| 6 pieces at a time (`final.py`: claim files, `--workers`, `STRATA_RENDER_WORKERS`) | 12 pieces: 425 s, 5.9 fps in all. |
| Seam band, parallax sampling and the per-pixel render on the GPU (`render/gpu.py`) | one process 4.7 fps; six together 7.6 fps. With six processes the CPU was the limit: ffmpeg's colour conversions (decoder YUV to rgb48, encoder rgb48 to YUV) took a core each, 18 ffmpeg processes. |
| Colour conversions on the GPU too (native p010 in, p010 out) | six together 11.2 fps (12 pieces in 222 s; the first test was 0.38 fps). |

## What changed in the pictures

- GPU against CPU on the same frames: PSNR 77 to 90 dB (16-bit), rounding only (bilinear/bicubic in float against cv2's fixed point).
- YUV to RGB on the GPU against swscale: mean difference 45 of 65535 codes, larger at the edges of saturated colours (different chroma upsampling; 0.7 percent of the pixels over 2000 codes in the test frame). Seam latitude can move by up to 0.6 degrees between the two inputs. Switch off with `STRATA_GPU_DECODE=0` (also keeps ffmpeg's RGB to YUV on the encode side) and the whole GPU renderer with `STRATA_GPU_RENDER=0`.
- Not on the GPU: DIS optical flow in the parallax measurement (OpenCV CPU, 17 ms), the grade, the overlay, transitions.

## Model trials on this machine (10 Oct 2026; scripts in `scripts/win_exp/`, models in `models/exp/`, gitignored)

**Enlarging** (`vsr_compare.py`, `vsr_set.py`): `edit/upscale.py` ran its model on the CPU on anything but a Mac; it now uses `hw.gpu_device()` (RealPLKSR 0.39 s a frame at 640x360 to 1080p). SeedVR2 3B fp8 (numz/ComfyUI-SeedVR2_VideoUpscaler CLI, weights `numz/SeedVR2_comfyUI`): 3.4 s a frame for 960x540 to 1080p, 12 s a frame for 1080p to 4K, with `--vae_encode_tiled --vae_decode_tiled` (512 px tiles), `--blocks_to_swap 20` and CPU offload devices; untiled it either ran out of memory (4K) or took 4 to 5 minutes a batch of 9 (1080p). On 14 clips (10 from the film, 4 street view) SeedVR2 gave the most natural detail everywhere there was something to recover; the project's default RealPLKSR draws crackle on foliage and wall, SCUNet then Lanczos is clean and soft, and nothing helps a lens covered by a finger. A faint regular grid shows on skin at 100%. Cost is the issue: 21 hours for the 252 s film at 4K, so it is a per-shot choice.

**Transcription** (`asr_compare.py`): Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 and Canary-1B-v2 (ONNX int8, onnx-asr) on the 171 existing segments of Legends. Against Whisper large-v3-turbo the word disagreement is about 30 percent for both (their output keeps "um" and repeats and spells numbers out, Whisper does not; Parakeet and Canary disagree with each other by the same amount). Each reproduces 5 of the 25 word changes the Gemini check made. Speed on 571 s of speech: Parakeet 28 s, Canary 60 s. Canary forced to the language Whisper detected on non-English segments (the "French" ones are mostly crowd noise) loops ("no, no, no ..."); Parakeet with automatic language gives short English words. No ground truth exists, so none is shown to be more accurate; `asr.json` has every segment's four texts for reading.

**Speech enhancement** (`enhance_run.py` in `.venv-cv`, `enhance_eval.py`): ClearerVoice MossFormer2_SE_48K, MossFormerGAN_SE_16K and FRCRN_SE_16K on the GPU (a 214 s clip in a few seconds), against the project's `audio_clean.flac` (DeepFilterNet3, capped) and the original, on 76 English segments of six clips. DNSMOS overall: original 2.10, current 2.47, MossFormer2 2.94, MossFormerGAN 3.04, FRCRN 3.02 (background 2.3, 2.8 and about 3.95). Whisper word probability and word error against the Gemini-corrected text do not move (0.78 to 0.80; 327 reference words). The current track is capped on purpose so the crowd and the place stay; the others remove them. NVIDIA's RE-USE needs mamba-ssm and Studio Voice is a container, neither runs on Windows here.

**Scene and object recognition** (`qwen_compare.py`): Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M with its vision projector through llama.cpp (winget, Vulkan; 20 GB, partly in system memory) against the Mac's Qwen3.5-9B-4bit answers, 96 views of 4 clips with the stage's own prompts: 3.7 s a picture for both prompts (the Mac took 8.7 s), every answer parsed. Agreement: weather 90 percent, water 97, crowd 94, lens problems 91, snow 100, setting 65, lighting 59. People counts rank the same (0.998); scenery scores are 1.5 points higher and rank 0.55 with the old ones; clarity 0.75. On 179 object crops the label is the same in 36 percent and shares a word in 64; looking at 24 that differ, the new model is better on some (trail marker, runner, drone as bird) and worse on others (calls fences hedges, dormer windows roofs) and answers "unclear" 8 times against none. Not clearly better for naming; faster than the Mac.

**Whisper** (`whisper_compare.py`): large-v3 against large-v3-turbo on the 171 Legends segments, on the GPU: 63 s against 54 s for 571 s of speech, the words differ by 26 percent, and large-v3 reproduces 7 of the 25 Gemini word corrections against 5. New projects on a CUDA machine now start with large-v3 (`hw.default_whisper_model`); Legends keeps turbo (changing it would re-run every transcript-dependent stage and move the saved word edits).

**Qwen3.5-9B on Windows** (three GGUF builds through llama.cpp, same 96 views and 179 crops): answers match the Mac's Qwen3.5-9B-4bit far better than the 35B-A3B did (setting 90 percent against 65, lens problems 92 to 97 against 91, object label identical 59 to 63 percent against 36, "unclear" 0 to 1 times against 8) at 1.2 to 2.1 s a picture against 8.7 s on the Mac. Q4_K_M, the size of the Mac's build, is as close as the 8-bit ones. `analysis/vlm.py` runs the scenes, objects and regions scripts through llama-server (Q4_K_M, fetched on first use) wherever mlx_vlm is not installed; one clip's scenes stage took 21 s here against 135 s on the Mac.

## Windows parity of the stages (11 Oct 2026)

Why six 4K render processes crashed: the GPU's 16 GB. Each process holds about 5.2 GB at 3840x2160 (2.6 GB at 1080p), six ran the card out of memory and the encoders died. `final.render_workers(size)` now takes the free GPU memory into account (2 at 4K, 6 at 1080p). Three processes at 4K 50 fps rendered 1392 frames in 191 s (7.3 fps, the whole 12,619-frame film in about 30 minutes); one process 5.4 fps.

A scratch project of two clips (`models/exp/winproj`, built with `open`) now runs every stage that needs no key or track on this PC: ingest, audio_extract, audio_clean, audio_events, audio, transcribe, align, exposure, motion, proxy, thumb, people, identity (with the Legends wearer profile), face_view, scenes, objects, speakers, candidates, cameras, lens, quality, audio_background (TIGER-DnR on CUDA), thumb_best. Not run: places, sun (need the race track), thumb_overlay (map key), transcript_check (Gemini).

What had to change: one environment instead of `.venv-vision` (`analysis/vlm.vision_python` falls back to the interpreter running the stage; `requirements-vision.txt`); `torchvision` from the CUDA index (the PyPI one has no CUDA `nms`) and the pinned OpenCV 4.12 back over the headless 5.0 that ultralytics installs; DeepFilterNet needs a Rust build, so `audio_clean` falls back to MossFormer2_SE_48K with the same attenuation cap (the original plus 10^(-cap/20) of what it removed); the sound classifier, ECAPA speaker model and YOLOE weights are fetched on first use and the classifier, ECAPA and TIGER run on CUDA; speechbrain copies instead of linking; the proxy decodes and encodes through the GPU colour conversion (0.27 to 0.21 s a frame measured over 240 frames of a long clip; the Mac does 0.07: the time is the per-frame CPU maths and the many small GPU round trips of a single process); the GPU duty cycle is 1.0 on CUDA (0.6 on a Mac, where it protects the screen). `doctor` reports the GPU.

Bigger detectors (`vision_models.py`, 40 views of Legends clip 0023, RTX 5070 Ti): YOLO11 pose s, m, l, x take 25, 22, 26 and 32 ms a view; the bigger ones find only people the small one finds too (m 94 percent of s, l and x 75 percent, with fewer detections at the same confidence). YOLOE-26 s, m, l take 51, 57 and 72 ms for both models and give 88, 83 and 50 named boxes; 76 to 80 percent of the bigger models' boxes are in the small one's. Time is not the constraint on this card; nothing here shows the bigger sizes finding more, and their confidences are calibrated differently, so the thresholds would need retuning. Kept the small sizes.

**Proxy and worker speed (11 Oct):** the whole scene render (lens projection, weights, seam, rescue, composition) now runs in one GPU call (`gpu.scene`), the lens bands come back in one transfer, the next frame pair is decoded and the finished picture converted and written by background threads, and the picture stays on the GPU between render and encoder: the proxy goes from 0.27 to 0.13 s a frame over 240 frames (the Mac does 0.07; what is left is the CPU optical flow of the parallax and the seam solver). A GPU-made and a CPU-made proxy of the same frames agree at 45 dB. A big NVIDIA machine (12+ cores, 32+ GB) now defaults to 4 workers of 4 threads (`resources.machine_defaults`), the light stages (proxy, exposure, speech recognition, alignment, speakers, quality) may run in several copies (`concurrent_caps`), and a worker waits for free GPU memory before a stage that needs it (`STAGE_VRAM_GB`, read with nvidia-smi).

**Local text model** (`text_models.py`, 20 real vocabulary prompts: scene-label category matching and word review): Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M (3B active, 20 GB, partly in memory; 1.0 s a prompt) follows the instructions better than Qwen3.5-9B (0.3 s a prompt): asked for at most 4 categories it gives 1 to 2 that fit (a road scene: town_village, road_traffic; the 9B adds forest_trail and others, up to 5), and it calls a trail marker and a deck distinct things where the 9B says no to both. It is the default text model (`vlm.llm_files`, STRATA_LLM_GGUF overrides; `models/qwen36-35b-a3b`); the scripts `vocab_llm.py` and `edit/llm_cli.py` use it through llama.cpp wherever MLX is not installed (14 s to start the server). Gemini stays the script writer.

## Openings and endings (11 Oct 2026; `edit/bookends.py`, `strata360 bookends FOLDER`)

The film had no designed first or last shot. `bookends` lists candidate windows from the analysis that is already there and renders previews (from the proxies; 960x540, 25 fps, a 2 x 2 contact picture each) into `<project>/strata360/bookends/`:

- **globe_face**: the little planet (260 degrees, stereographic, looking down) opens onto a close view of your face (an opening) or closes over it (an ending). Windows are where the face view score stays high (mean 0.8, no second under 0.5), weighted by steadiness and scenery, from the first or last half of the race.
- **crowd**: a push in or a pan across a busy place, where the scenes stage saw 6 or more people in the front view; openings from the first fifth of the clips, endings from the last fifth.
- **sequence**: one continuous camera move in one moment of the footage: the globe opens (2.0 s), the camera pans 270 degrees round the crowd (3.2 s), and closes in on your face (2.4 s, 7.6 s in all); needs a clear face at the end and a crowd in the 3 s before it.
All three are the film's own camera paths (`edit/framing.resolve_segment` for the face and crowd moves, keyframes with fov, dist and disc for the globe), so they render through the normal pipeline and animated text can go over them. The local vision model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B with its projector) is shown the contact pictures of each group in one request and asked to score them and pick the best, with reasons; the answer is stored in `bookends.json`. On Legends it found 19 candidates and 3 chosen openings (the sequence on clip 0004 at 15.8 s scored 9 of 10; the model rejected one candidate for being in a dorm room and another for a finger over the lens) and one ending (clip 0024); the finish has no crowd in the scene labels, so no crowd or sequence ending. Not done yet: putting a chosen bookend into the plan (the planner is beat and voice-over driven), the music and voice-over around it, and the animated text.

## Text through the film: subtitles, word art, options and placement (Windows work, October)

- **Subtitles**: `render/captions.py` builds cues from the voice-over timings and the transcripts; preview and final burn them in and write `subtitles.srt` / `film.srt`.
- **Word art**: `render/titles.py` draws designed text (fonts in `render/title_fonts/`, glow/shadow/extrude/stroke, per-character animation). `edit/title_design.py` has the local vision LLM compose it for the shot, then `enforce` fixes timing and placement.
- **Options and the planner**: `edit/film_text.py` (`strata360 filmtext`) has the text LLM propose quotes and progress updates (checked against the facts and transcript), a vision pass rates each against its shot (excitement, risk of covering it), the best get their word art placed, and the planner picks from the combined score.
- **Placement is an optimisation, not a rule**: each word takes the size and position of least cost (faces 10, other text 8, other words 12, main person 1.2, a little for shrinking and moving), coordinate descent over all words. After placement `blocking()` reports face / subject / text shares and a 0-10 score with a note; the score is part of the planner's score (25%), so a card that still blocks something can lose to another, but nothing is vetoed. On the Legends test the four picked cards cleared every face; two still overlap part of the body (blocking 0.6 to 1.1).
