## Generated music, first real-audio runs (6 Oct 2026, Legends track on the Mac)

First time G0 ran on a real track (Demucs 4.0.1 installed in `.venv`, reusing torch 2.9.1; `htdemucs_ft` separates the 4-minute track in about 145 s at low priority, 4 models of 80 MB cached in `~/.cache/torch`). Listened to by the user (5-minute edits with an intro, quiet and full stretches, vocals and two endings): "sounds good". Peak GPU/memory not recorded.
- **Learned:** the bar similarity saturated on real music (mean 0.99: every join looked free). Bar features are now centred across the track's bars (`music.bar_features`; the grid file is version 3). Mean is 0.26 on Legends.
- **Learned:** the beat tracker follows onsets, so in dense stretches beats landed on off-beat hits (56 irregular intervals of the 400, bars from 2.15 to 2.69 s at 97 BPM). `music.regularise` pulls a beat back onto the midpoint of its neighbours when it is more than 40 ms off: bar standard deviation 0.089 to 0.047 s, irregular intervals 15. The first six bars (a quiet intro) are still uneven; the opening is played as it is, so it is not used for joins.
- **Learned:** "quiet" made by turning drums and bass off leaves only Demucs's `other` stem, which sounds thin and processed. Quiet now uses the track's own quiet bars untouched (`layers.as_is_bars`) and only thins bars louder than asked (stems at 30/50/80 percent, mix 3 dB lower).
- **Learned:** vocal gain ramps centred on a bar line let 0.125 s of singing into the next bar (read as -23 to -34 dB); vocal ramps are now short and a fall ends on the line (-33 to -37 dB, nothing audible).
- **Built:** keep the opening (first build and break, `music_build.intro_bars`) as the track has it; vocal windows grow to whole sung stretches (`layers.snap_windows`) and the plan holds those stretches unbroken (`remix.plan(hold=...)`), a window that cannot be whole stays as asked (`partial_windows`); a quick ending (`ending='quick'`: the track up to its last loud bar, 3 s fade) next to the track's own long fade; joins worse than 0.3 are charged extra (`bad_join`).
- **Learned:** with the join penalty the planner looped a cheap 2 to 3 bar phrase four or five times. A penalty on how often a bar is played re-routed the whole plan (worst join up from 0.12 to 0.35) and still allowed the loop; a penalty on short runs works: leaving a stretch after fewer than 4 bars costs up to 0.3 extra (`remix.plan(min_run, short_weight)`, the state carries the stretch length). Both Legends edits then have every stretch at 4 bars or more and worst joins of 0.2.
- **Not yet:** one verse could not be played whole in the 5-minute edit; none of this is wired to the director's `sing` item or `music.json`.

- **Built (6 Oct): the director's `sing` item** (docs/ai-music.md 4.2a): `script_pack.music_facts` gives each trusted phrase an id and a `hook` flag (the same words sung more than once); the writer's prompt (version 15) has the item and its rules (at most 4, 30 s apart, never doubtful, a hook only repeated far apart); `script_draft.check` sends back an unknown or doubtful phrase, too many, too close, a repeat that is not the hook, and, with the fixed track, a `sing` item not anchored at the bar a lead-in before where the track sings it; its length is the phrase rounded up to bars plus a bar each side (`sing_seconds`); `director_notes` flags the hook before the halfway point; `script_plan.pieces` plans it as picture and remembers the phrase; the web script panel shows it. **Not run against a real model yet** (a paid Gemini call): the first real director run with it needs a look.

## Local upscaling of low-resolution shots, and a still of the final film (8 Oct 2026)

Question: can a local model add detail where the output holds more pixels than the source (tight zooms on the footage, the 3840-wide standard Google panoramas at 10.7 px/deg against the 22 px/deg a 1080p view at 85 degrees needs)? Built as `edit/upscale.py`: opt-in `upscale=True` on `streetview_cam.render`, and `--upscale` / the "Enlarge tight shots" box for the final film and the stills (`render/final.py`, `render/still.py`).
- **Learned: judge by eye, not by PSNR.** Against the real hi-res Google panoramas every learned model scored worse than plain scaling (PSNR 22 to 27 against 29 dB) while looking better: the scores reward smoothness and punish invented texture. The pictures are the test.
- **Learned: the first model chosen was the worst for fine detail.** Real-ESRGAN `realesr-general-x4v3` (fast, 0.6 s a 960x540 picture) erased the lettering on a bus stop sign that the plain scaler still hinted at. On that crop SwinIR-M real-world GAN and Real-ESRGAN x4plus kept text-like marks, RealPLKSR (Phhofm `4xNomosWebPhoto_RealPLKSR`, web photos with noise and JPEG) came closest to the real hi-res ("BUS" and a number readable) and is the default. Seconds a 960x540 picture, alone on the GPU: compact models 0.3 (C3 too soft, R invents terrain), general 0.6, RealPLKSR 4.6, x4plus 9, SwinIR 28, DAT2 43 (blocky). A nine-picture street view clip: SwinIR 949 s, RealPLKSR 96 s. None recovers text that is not in the source: the real fix where it matters is the 'hi' Google grid.
- **Learned: night footage does not benefit, even with the stronger models** or with the exposure lifted first (an invertible shadow lift, gain 6): the camera's own noise reduction has already smeared the dark parts, and the compact R model invents texture. `upscale.skip_reason` reuses the object detector's night test (`analysis.objects.clip_is_dark`); on this race no clip has `sun.json` yet (the sun stage has not run), so it excludes nothing until it does (exposure alone would flag 0006, median 0.002).
- **Built: one factor per shot, and only the part of a panorama the shot looks at.** The factor comes from the tightest zoom anywhere in the section (`plan_zoom`, `upscale.factor_for`), so a zoom that is tight for a moment still enlarges every picture the same. Each panorama is cropped to the window its views reach (`picture_window`, the widest view, the plan's yaws, a margin) and only that is enlarged (about 17 percent of the sphere for the test section) and cached (`<picture>.w<x0>_<x1>_<y0>_<y1>.<model>.x<factor>.jpg`); `reproject` reads a `Windowed` picture.
- **Built: `render/still.py`, a still of the final film at a film time** (CLI `strata360 still`, `/api/film/still`, a "Final still" control with a size dropdown under the film preview player). It runs the film's own pipeline for the piece that holds the frame (renderer, exposure match, overlay, transitions; four warm-up frames inside a plain stretch so the seam settles). 1080p about 10 s and 4K 15 s once a clip's data is cached; the first still of a clip took 9 minutes (one-off preparation of its data), which the page says.
- **Built (8 Oct): enlarging in the film pipeline.** `FinalSource.shot_factor` gives each shot ONE factor from the narrowest field of view on its camera path (`upscale.factor_for(NATIVE_PPD 20, output width, fov)`), none for generated clips and night footage (`upscale.skip_reason`). A shot is rendered at 1/factor of the output size, enlarged by the model in the film's 16-bit RGB values (no banding), fitted to the output, then graded and given the overlay at full size (the overlay text stays sharp). The film's key includes the setting (`final_key(..., upscale)`), so an enlarged film has its own folder. A still skips the model, grade and overlay for its warm-up frames (29 s down to 13 s for a 1080p still).
- **Learned: at 4K almost the whole film would be enlarged, which is not practical.** Under the Legends plan 165 of the 193 footage seconds need x2 or x3 at 3840 wide (even a 90 degree view holds about half the pixels a 4K frame wants: the accepted trade-off of README 8.10), against 3 shots (x2) at 1080p. A frame costs 4 s or more through the model, so a 4K film at 50 frames a second would take many hours; use it for stills, 1080p or chosen shots. Not decided: a threshold or per-shot choice for how tight a shot must be before it is enlarged.
- **Built (8 Oct): progress and timings on the page.** `render/progress.py` writes `progress.json` (the film: `final/<key>/`; a still: beside the picture): the steps in order with their times (prepare, load model, render, timemap, audio, assemble; for a still prepare, load model, render, write), the time spent inside the render by kind of work (reading the video, joining the lenses, projecting, enlarging, exposure match, overlay, encoding) and a log; `/api/final` and `/api/film/still` pass it on and `RenderProgress.tsx` shows it (the running step counts up live). First real still with enlarging at 1080p: prepare 1.5 s, load model 1.1 s, render 8.3 s (reading the video 2.3, joining the lenses 0.8, enlarging 4.3, overlay 0.7), write 0.1 s.
- **Built (8 Oct): enlarge modes, auto series and a gallery of all stills.** "Enlarge" is a mode (`edit/upscale.py` `MODES`): `off`; `1080p` or `1440p` (the model works up to that width, then a standard Lanczos resample to the output size); `full` (the model all the way). The factor is worked out for the mode's width, so a shot a lower mode already satisfies is not touched (a 68 degree shot at 4K: x2 for 1440p, nothing for 1080p). Real run, 4K still of a 58 degree shot: no enlarging 16 s for two stills, model to 1080p 18 s, to 1440p 30 s (enlarging 4.8 s a frame at 1080p; the resample 0.01 s); the 1080p and 1440p results look close and both show more detail than none. The Final still control has the mode dropdown and an **Auto** button: `render/still.py` `pick_moments` takes 4 moments for a film of 2 minutes rising evenly to 10 at 30 minutes (`moment_count`), one from each equal stretch of the film, choosing the shot that adds most that is new (clip, tightness, generated or footage); for the Legends plan: a wide footage shot, a generated street view clip, a medium and a tight shot. One worker renders a batch one after the other (`still --t A --t B ...`: the model and the clips' data stay loaded; one failure does not stop the rest). Every still keeps a `<name>.png.json` (time, frame, size, mode, model, the shots with their narrowest view and factor and why a shot was not enlarged, when it was made, how long it took, steps and time by kind of work); `/api/film/stills` lists them all (flagging ones from an older plan) with the renders in progress, `/api/film/still/thumb` serves small JPEGs, and `StillGallery.tsx` is the grid, a viewer (arrow keys, Escape, all the details) and a side by side comparison of up to four at full size with shared scrolling.
- **Tried (8 Oct): diffusion and video upscalers on the 16 GB Mac, blocked by the resource guard.** Research: FlashVSR needs a CUDA block-sparse attention backend (no Mac support found); the Apple Silicon route is SeedVR2 through the MLX port (`mlx-gen` 0.38, MIT; SeedVR2-3B weights Apache-2.0, `AbstractFramework/seedvr2-3b-8bit` and `-4bit` downloaded; installed in its own environment outside the repo). Two guarded attempts (`strata360.pipeline.guard.heavy`, 5 and 4 GB declared) on a 960x540 and a 480x270 street view picture were stopped: the first refused to start (7.6 GB free, 11 needed with the reserve), the second and third were killed within seconds when the system reached memory pressure level 4, and after them the guard refused to start at all because 4.7 GB of swap was in use against its 4.3 GB limit (stale swap, the same pattern as 4 Oct). No picture was made, nothing was left running, and the guard was not switched off. Decision needed: relax the limits for this experiment, clear the swap (restart or purge), or run the models on another machine. Untested: OSEDiff / TADSR (Stable Diffusion based, probably a similar footprint), SparkVSR, Upscale-A-Video.
- **Not yet:** no run on a diffusion or video model (SeedVR2, FlashVSR, one-step diffusion); shimmer between frames of an enlarged shot has been looked at on single frames only (no enlarged film has been rendered); the Street view page does not set `upscale` or the hires choice; the progress panel has been tested with mocks, not looked at in a browser.

## Enlarger comparison on the problem close views (9 Oct 2026, Legends film times 120.6, 139.8 and 178.1 s)

- **Setup:** the same three stills rendered by the film pipeline at 4K direct (no model) and at 1080p then enlarged 2x three ways (Lanczos, RealPLKSR `4xNomosWebPhoto`, SwinIR-M BSRGAN GAN), 800x500 crops at 4K scale. Only these two models are on disk (the Real-ESRGAN weights from the earlier street view comparison are not). Times for a 1920x1080 picture to 2x: RealPLKSR 16 to 18 s, SwinIR 110 s.
- **Learned: the problem is the source, not the resampling.** On the close you-views the OSV's own compression has blocked and smeared the skin and the ground; 4K direct and Lanczos look the same. **RealPLKSR, the default, is the worst of the three here:** it turns the codec's block patterns on the ground and the jacket seam into worm-like invented texture and adds grain to skin. **SwinIR is cleanest** (smooth, no invented texture, eyelashes kept) but plastic and 110 s a frame, which is not usable for a film. So the model chosen on the street view crops (lettering) is not the right one for faces in compressed 360 footage.
- **Next:** try artefact removal (deblock, denoise) before or instead of super-resolution, and the other models of the earlier comparison; needs weights downloaded.

Grids in `final/stills/upscale-compare/maxzoom/fgrid_*.png`. **Learned:** RealPLKSR on the dry grass and ground at 3x adds crisp blades with moderate noise (the look the user prefers); half-strength SCUNet then RealPLKSR is the crispest but etched and web-like on fine texture; half-strength SCUNet then general is clean and natural but soft; general alone is soft; skin stays flat with every model (the face has no detail in the source to find).
- **Street view at max zoom (6 panoramas with both the standard 10.7 pixels a degree and the high 21 pixels a degree grids, a 55 degree view to 3840x2160, about 6.4x from the standard grid; the high grid reprojected is the reference; the standard panorama window enlarged 4x then reprojected):** grids `sgrid_*.png`. **Learned:** RealPLKSR is over-cooked here (sharpness 5 to 15 times the reference's, scribbled leaf edges, darker and more contrasty than the reference); Real-ESRGAN general is the closest to the reference in tone and sharpness (0.8 to 1.6 times); half-strength SCUNet versions are in between. PSNR against the reference ranks Lanczos first (22 to 26 dB) because it is soft; it is not a quality ranking.

- **Zoom histogram (9 Oct 2026, `scripts/zoom_histogram.py`, chart `final/stills/upscale-compare/zoom_histogram.png`):** zoom = output pixels a degree at 4K over the camera's 20 a degree, in 0.25x bins, by shot (the narrowest view of each shot) and by moment (seconds of film sampled along every camera path). ## Selective enlarging built: 2.5x, RealPLKSR only (9 Oct 2026, decided with the user)

- **Rule:** a shot is enlarged, all of it, only when more than 0.5 s of its camera path needs at least 2.5x the source's pixels per degree (at the film's width, so 3840 wide: a view of 76.8 degrees or tighter). RealPLKSR is the only model (the default `MODEL`). On Legends that is 6 shots, 40.2 s, 2,010 frames: the three 58 degree close views and the three 68 degree dialogue views (about 4 hours estimated on the Mac, not timed); the 85 degree views (2.26x) and the 80 degree ones (2.4x) are left as they are. Night footage is still never enlarged, and neither are generated clips.
- **Code:** `edit/upscale.py` `MIN_ZOOM`, `MIN_SECONDS` and `seconds_over` (the path read every 0.05 s); `FinalSource.shot_factor` applies it after the narrowest-view factor (the shot's note says "only N s of it need 2.5x or more" when it is left alone); `strata360 final` and `strata360 still` take `--upscale-min-zoom X` (default 2.5; the mode, `--upscale full` for the tight shots, still sets how far the model works); the film's key includes the threshold. Checked on real stills: 120.6 s (58 degrees) enlarged x3, 63.4 s (85 degrees) not. Tests: `TestSelective` in `tests/unit/test_final_upscale.py` (1909 unit and the render integration tests pass).
- **Not yet:** an enlarged film has not been rendered; the Final film panel has no setting for the threshold; shimmer between frames of an enlarged shot is unchecked.
